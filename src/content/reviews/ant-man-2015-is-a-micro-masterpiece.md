# Greetings. I am setting out to review all of the MCU movies before Avengers: Doomsday (2026). For the list, I started out with Iron Man (2008). The next film on the docket is **Ant-Man (2015)**. This is the 12th MCU movie and the final one of Phase 2. But did it go out on a good note?

# **Story**

# When I think of Ant-Man, I see an underdog story. Yes, this was the case with Captain American: The First Avenger (2011) and Guardians Of The Galaxy (2014), but I feel like they walked so this movie could run. What I mean by that is that this is the movie that feels like the biggest underdog story, with the highest stakes, and most morally flawed protagonist. This means that the pacing must be great, and I feel like it is. This is one of the MCU’s shortest movies, but the scenes take exactly as long as they should. Action scenes are quick and dialogue scenes are longer. The heart accompanies the story a lot, as well as Scott’s character arc. The emotional aspect is a prominent part of Scott’s arc, as it turns him into a better person. This results in me smiling at multiple scenes, and caring about Mr. Lang as a character. Another thing the plot does right is being different from the rest of the MCU. Not just at being more filled with comedy, but being a heist movie, and making Scott… somewhat of an antagonist. But then again, it still feels like an MCU movie, with action scenes and side characters such as The Falcon. However, I do have two issues. One: the film is overwhelmingly predictable. You have the protagonist not taking things seriously. You have the happy ending that can feel rushed to at times. You have the villain who wants the protagonist dead. Everything, it plays very straight. My other gripe is that the comedy is a mixed bag. You might sometimes laugh at the banter between Ant-Man and Luis, but some of the dialogue jokes are rather irritating to me. **VERDICT: 16/20**

# **Visuals**

# This was the first MCU film to have set designers make the sets noticeably smaller to make it seem like the actors shrunk sizes. Not only is it very important to Scott’s superpowers, but it looks timeless. Every set looks like it could be made today, and when watching this with my younger brother, I actually thought Paul Rudd shrunk. As well, there is a noticeable attention to detail. For instance, when Yellowjacket and Ant-Man fight in Cassie’s room, they throw Thomas the Train toys at each other. It makes sense for it to injure them a lot, too because they are about the size of the objects during these scenes. The action and cinematography is also incredible. Starting with the cinematography, the camera rotates slowly and focuses on every attention to detail. The action is as well creative. I have no idea why, but I find the action more enticing than most because these characters are shrunken down in this movie. Maybe because it is more creative than most Marvel movies? The color scheme tends to Yellow, to match the comedy. Yes, it looks ridiculous, but so is this movie. So that is a good thing here. And then you have the CGI ant models. Each ant looks amazing, and elaborately detailed. My only real gripe is that Ant-Man’s suit looks… out of character for him. It makes him feel really angry, when that is not the case. **VERDICT: 19/20**

# **Characters**

# As I said before, Ant-Man is an underdog story. So an underdog story needs an underdog protagonist, and Scott “Ant-Man” Lang delivers that in spades. Ant-Man is a charismatic thief with good intentions. He went to prison for stealing money from his boss and nearly giving it to overcharged customers. However, Lang has good qualities, such as attending his daughter’s birthday party and even getting her a gift, and staying loyal to Luis and the boys. His character arc on becoming a better person is well-paced and heavily realized. Luis is his best friend, and I find his loyalty very charming. However, whenever he gets into an argument with Ant-Man, their relationship feels real. Officer Bill Paxton and Mrs. Maggie Lang are his wife and replacement, and I thought they would just welcome him back with open arms. Boy, was I wrong! They are cynical and learn to accept him, which is amazing character development. Casandra “Cassie” Lang is his daughter. She is one of the most adorable characters in the franchise, and is a great catalyst for her daddy’s character arc. That means she just had to be in the entire movie. Hank Pym is up next, and he is a character that I surprisingly enjoy! What I love about him is that he has the same character arc of Ant-Man. They both worry if they are good fathers. However, there are two characters that need improvement. Hope “Wasp” Van Dyne is a charming character, but being enjoyable only goes so far. Especially if you are just a plot device in the movie, which is the case for Hope. Also, Darren “Yellowjacket” Cross is a rather poor villain. Yes, he serves as a threat, but he feels just like a character who hates the protagonist. **VERDICT: 17/20**

# **Acting**

# If you had to describe Paul Rudd as Scott “Ant-Man” Lang, how would you in one word? For me it would be charming. Rudd has this charisma and vibe that I cannot describe. All I can say is that it feels real, because he also sells the serious moments. Michael Peña is Luis. Peña is a real fast talker here, and that works very well within his character. Also, he does an amazing job at just being humorous. Evangeline Lilly is Hope “Wasp” Van Dyne. Lilly portrays Wasp with a real edge, having strong chemistry with Rudd and Douglas. Judy Greer, Bobby Cannavale and Abby Ryder Fortson have excellent chemistry with Rudd and each other as Maggie, Bill and Cassie. Michael Douglas is Hank Pym. What I love about Douglas’ performance is that he rolls authority, sharpness and gravitas in an amazing portrayal. Even Corey Stoll as Darren “Yellowjacket” Cross gives a great performance. Stoll gives a spirited portrayal and a fear-inducing tone. **VERDICT: 20/20**

# The movie’s score is wonderful. It features a catchy, fast-paced 7/4 march main theme that dynamically mirrors the character's shrinking and growing. I feel like the sound effect is absolutely perfect for this tune. Also what was incorporated is a 1960s spy-caper jazz and surf-rock influences to match the film's caper and heist tone. It sounds pompous and full of energy. The comedy is quick and catchy, while the softer scenes have less beats. My only gripe is that I wish there were more memorable songs. **VERDICT: 18/20**

# **In conclusion**, Ant-Man (2015) is an amazing movie that I would recommend to any heist movie fan. It is a great movie in terms of writing, acting, sound and look. In fact, this is one of the greatest MCU movies of all time great phase. **Final Score: 90%**
