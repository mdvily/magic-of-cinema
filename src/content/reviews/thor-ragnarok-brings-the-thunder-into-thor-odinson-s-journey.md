# Greetings. I am setting out to review all of the MCU movies before Avengers: Doomsday (2026). For the list, I started out with Iron Man (2008). The next film on the docket is Thor 3: Ragnarok (2017). This movie is widely considered a massive improvement over the second movie. But is it so? **SPOILERS!**

# **Story**

# The story is absolutely amazing. And that is for so many reasons. I think the greatest reason is the comedy. I keep laughing at every joke, because they are consistently memorable and it makes sense for these certain characters to tell them. Especially that Loki snake prank backstory. I consistently laugh at the jokes, but cry at the heart. I will admit, the film can be highly tonally inconsistent. However, the emotional factor is still very powerful. I cry when Thor and Loki approach Odin one last time, because it was built up. Another thing is that I surprisingly love the pacing. The movie takes as long as the scenes should, and then casually moves onto the next. Like when the Odinsons meet Hela Odinstottir. You also have the climax subversion. Mostly, during the scene where Thor defeats his sister by outsmarting her. Mostly, the antagonists get overpowered. Not this time. **VERDICT: 18/20**

# **Visuals**

# This movie is a grand slam improvement over Thor 2: The Dark World in every way, especially with the visuals. I mean it went from a 0/10 to a 10/10 in just one movie! First off, the action scenes are amazing! Such as… Thor’s first battle with Surtur, his clash with Hulk and the electric final battle. Every scene has a memorable moment, and excellent choreography and cinematography. Speaking of which, the camera moves slowly, which gets you in this world. But it moves quickly during the arena sequence. You then have the color. The second movie looks ugly as heck with just a crappy amount of gray. The third movie looks beautiful as heck with just a fantastic amount of rainbow. Especially in Sakaar. Every shot there is vibrant and full of life. Also, the character modeling and costumes is the best of Thor’s story. Especially Surtur’s design. It looks so elaborate, but tangible. Every attention to detail looks great and well-worked on, such as his large horns and yellow eyes. **VERDICT: 20/20**

# **Characters**

# The characters are honestly my least favorite aspect of the film. It is great, but it could be better. Let us start off with the characters I disliked. Hulk and Hiemdall. Hear me out! Hulk has an awfully rushed and unfocused character arc, whatsoever. Dr. Bruce Banner starts off wanting to be human, and accepts himself as the Hulk and ends up the exact same way. And I never really liked Heimdall as a character. I feel like he just has no narrative connection to the story. They just put him in for the sake of it. Now for the ones I do like! Thor is an awesome protagonist once again. He is incredibly charming and has a strong and well-paced character arc of realizing who he is. I just wish that they took him more seriously. Loki is the most charming of the bunch, and I already explained in previous movies that he is an awesome character. But here, he reaches his peak. He has a strong character arc of being a kinder version of himself. Hela is the villain, and honestly one of my favorite female antagonists of the entire MCU. She is scary, her backstory adds a lot of lore to Asgard, and her desire to better Asgard, after Odin’s death makes sense. There are other side characters such as Korg, Skurge and The Grandmaster that mostly add by being humorous. It works because they complement the tone very well and are actually very funny. Lastly, Valkyrie is the character with the strongest arc. She learns to move on from the loss of her kingdom and finally face Hela. **VERDICT: 17/20**

# **Acting**

# Chris Hemsworth is Thor Odinson. Hemsworth is absolutely hilarious in this role, as he is in many. But this time, Mr. Hemsworth reaches a new level. I laughed at every single joke he made, but whenever he has a more serious beat, you know I am here for it. Especially when he asks what he has done, as Ragnorök happens. Tom Hiddleston is Loki. Hiddleston is one of the most charming male actors out there, and this is no exception. He is so enjoyable, but he actually has great chemistry with Thor. They feel like actual brothers, and every time he puts out that smile, I laugh my bottom off. Cate Blanchett is Hela. Blanchett is one of the best actresses of her generation. And this is why. She perfectly gets the mixture between regality and horror. Tessa Thompson is Valkyrie. Thompson is a tough character here, but beneath that, she has a clear heart. Mark Ruffalo is The Hulk. Ruffalo captures the intelligence of The Hulk so well, but at the same time, he has you covered in the humor department. Taika Waititi, the movie’s director is Korg. Watiti is incredibly charming, but I really enjoy his chemistry with the others. Jeff Goldblum is The Grandmaster. Only Goldblum can balance a fear factor with high energy, humorous comedy this well! Karl Urban is Skurge. Urban is hilarious in this role, but gets the serious beats right. Especially when he goes to defend Asgard from Hela. Sir Idris Elba is Heimdall. Elba is a great actor, no denying. I just wish he had more personality here. I feel like he just says his lines, not means them. **VERDICT: 19/20**

# **Music**

# As you can remember with Thor 2: The Dark World (2013), I referred the music as decent. But this one is a flow-state improvement. One: the music fits that perfect blend of being loud enough, yet quiet enough. Especially when Thor and Loki meet their sister for the first time. I could hear Hela’s command to kneel, but also the music that followed, for an example. Two: During scenes like the Odinson brothers’ “Get Help” plan, the score leans more into a silly tone. Not only is it funny, but it gets right very well what this film tends to lean on: the humor. But during the scene where Asgard is destroyed, the film uses low, rumbling orchestral textures, heavy brass clusters, and deep, imposing male choir/voices. But then, the score slows down, when Hiemdall comforts Thor, saying he did what he had to do. Three: each character has a memorable theme. Valkyrie possesses a chanting orchestra, Hela has a low brass theme and Thor has a high-energy, upbeat tune. Four: what causes the film to truly present itself wonderfully is the instruments used and people involved, such as a choir and orchestra. Also, when they play “Immigrant Song”, I get taken back. **VERDICT: 20/20**
