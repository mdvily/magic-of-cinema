# Greetings. I am setting out to review all of the MCU movies before Avengers: Doomsday (2026). For the list, I started out with Iron Man (2008). The next film on the docket is Avengers 4: Endgame (2019). This film was planned alongside Avengers: Infinity War starting in 2014, and the two films were designed to conclude the 22-film Infinity Saga and wrap up the character arcs of Tony Stark, Steve Rogers and others. And so that the public would never know about this film, they disguised it as Avengers: Infinity War Part Two. It was not until the December of 2018 when this film was announced that title. But why was this a two-parter? While they were shooting Infinity War, Endgame’s script was not completed yet. And as for those Quantum suits? There was little to no time to finish creating them, so they were made by VFX. And lastly, some actors were deceived into joining scenes, such as Tom Holland (Spider-Man) thinking he was filming a wedding scene, but it was Stark’s funeral. Then, on April 26, 2019, Avengers: Endgame was finally released to conclude the Infinity Saga. So did Joseph and Anthony Russo stick the landing, or should this movie have been snapped away? **SPOILERS ALERT!**

# **Story**

# Why do many people talk about Avengers Endgame? Is it because this is currently the single highest grossing movie of all time? Or because it took us from one MCU saga to another? For me, it is that this movie is an absolute miracle. And the storytelling is a clear part of that. One of the reasons is the messaging of the film. The main theme is never to give up hope. I personally think that that is a fantastic moral, because of how integral it is to the plot. The Avengers keep believing that if they do this right and give it their all, Thanos’ gauntlet snap can be thwarted, and the deceased may return. It actually resonates with me for a reason you would not expect. When I was in seventh grade, I almost wanted to drop out of school, because nothing made sense? So I watched this movie, and was taught to keep working hard and believed in myself, and I made it through the next year with honors every semester. And one other thing is that it explains the plot holes. Such as, how is Nebula still alive if she killed her past self? That was a past version of a different universe. What happens to the present if the past gets changed? A new future emerges. Something else I enjoy is the tone. The film gets very serious and emotional, especially during Natasha’s death, Tony’s sacrifice and Sam receiving Cap’s shield. I cried during these moments, but for the jokes, like fat Thor, I laughed my head off. Many people hated this joke, and I am not one of them. I feel like it was accurate to his character. His main traits at fat Thor were approachable and caring, something that has always been the case for him. But my favorite aspect of the plot is the pacing. We start off with a devastating universe, where Thanos succeeded, then work our way to the best memory trip yet, as we explore the greatest Infinity Stone moments, and finally, the third act. It is the most satisfying thing ever. When I saw half of the population come back to life, it felt similar to being released from prison. And when Captain America says two famous words, that was the absolute cherry on top. **VERDICT: 20/20**

# **Visuals**

# Do you ever look at a movie and say, “Its visuals could be better”? Not for me with this one! The Avengers’ fourth installment looks absolutely legendary, and I love how much hard work everyone put into it. I choose to begin with the superhero suits, as they perfectly reflect onto the story and the characters. For instance, with the superhero suits. Iron Man’s suit and Hulk on Banner look perfect to their characters, rather it be Stark’s hard work with nanotechnology, or Banner accepting himself. Also, a lot of people thought Hulk’s design was stupid here. What do you mean? I think it looks wonderful! Hulk looks muscular, which fits his strength, but he also wears glasses, which affect our knowledge of his nerdiness. The CGI and VFX in general, though, is some of the best Marvel has ever made, even outside of the MCU. From as the Quantum suits to Captain Marvel’s proton energy or Thanos’ design, every piece of non-practical filmmaking looks magnificent. My second to last praise is the color. It is all over the rainbow, which perfectly demonstrates how beautiful a superhero movie can be, when it is worked hard on. But the real reason why I love this absolute visual spectacle is the action. Every single action segment is fantastic, from Captain America dueling himself, Black Widow and Hawkeye fighting to sacrifice themselves, and absolutely the final battle. Each scene has perfect cinematography and choreography, so it gets a perfect score. **VERDICT: 20/20**

# **Characters**

# This movie could have sucked regardless if it had poor character stories- the character arcs are all fantastic. Each and every character either has a perfect character arc, contributes everything they can to this story, or both. Tony “Iron Man” Stark is still that charming man we know and love, but he has what is my favorite character arc in the entirety of Marvel. And this film concludes it in the best possible way. He finally learns to put others above himself, and feeds us the hardest pill to swallow: his sacrifice. He slowly realizes how important others are as well, and his character arc comes full circle here. But Steve “Captain America” Rogers has another fantastic arc that mirrors Stark. Rogers finally puts himself above others, staying with Peggy in the ending. And after he slowly realizes that he as well deserves a happy ending, it is incredibly satisfying. Thor and Hulk are highly despised in this movie, and I think that is so unfair. Yes, fans did not like how they always try to be comedic, but that is who they always were. If they became entirely serious, it would feel weird, would it not? And they both have extremely meaningful character arcs. Thor overcomes his depression over the loss of his entire family, and Hulk finally achieves emotional balance, which is incredibly satisfying to me. Black Widow and Hawkeye learn to let their redemption turn them into better people. This is with Hawkeye using his family’s death not to become a criminal named Ronin, but to work hard for his bloodline to return. And Black Widow finally accepts the Avengers as her family, and gets the red out of her ledger. Lastly, Nebula and Rocket have character arcs of bonding with each other, long after their team’s demise. It is flawed, but it feels real. Even Thanos was incredible here. Yes, I am aware that he now wants to destroy and recreate the entire universe, which did not match his previous motivation. But that is the point! His motivation is not supposed to! Instead, it works perfectly as an evolution. Even the non-developing characters have a purpose. Ant-Man introduces his team to time travel, Captain Marvel (Who actually works here) helps to take down Thanos, and Rhodes keeps his best friend on the team, despite his initial refusal. **VERDICT: 20/20**

# **Acting**

# Every single actor gives it their all here, and never holds back. Robert Downey Jr. is Tony “Iron Man” Stark. Downey Jr. keeps on delivering hard-hitting performances as Stark, and this movie is exactly what I am talking about. When he says, “And I… am… Iron Man,” I was both amazed, and devastated. Chris Evans is Steve “Captain America” Rogers. Evans is the greatest person who could play Rogers, because of how darn well he does. What I love about him is the range he can convey. He can have both joyful scenes, when Sam says “On your left”, and upsetting ones, when he argues with Stark. For the comedic actors, we have Mark Ruffalo and Chris Hemsworth as Bruce “Hulk” Banner and Thor Odinson. I loved these roles because they both perfectly capture the comedic factor, all while still having a lot of emotion, rather it be happy or miserable. Scarlett Johansson and Jeremy Renner are Natasha “Black Widow” Romanoff and Clint “Hawkeye” Barton. Johansson and Renner both have the coolest body movements and the most emotional voices. Especially when Johansson says to Renner, “Let me go. It’s okay,” where I cannot keep my tears in. Karen Gillan and Bradley Cooper are Nebula and Rocket Raccoon. Both of them are absolutely sensational in the emotional department, where every word tugs at my heart strings. But at the same time, whenever they tell a joke, I laugh my buttocks off. Finally, Josh Brolin is Thanos. Brolin is not just scary, he is absolutely terrifying in this film. He walks slowly, has a raspy voice and entirely transforms into this titan. Every possible detail, he, along with every other actor and actress, have figured out in order to make their characters feel as real as possible. **VERDICT: 20/20**

# **Music**

# Honestly, the music is the culmination of every other fantastic track of the entire MCU, wrapped into the greatest burrito you will ever eat. In other words, I absolutely love the score here. First off, the volume is not too quiet, nor too loud. Every scene has a perfect tune, especially when Natasha’s final words leave her mouth. I could still hear them, but also the music. Another thing I love is how the score perfectly captures every single scene. It is intense when the team finds out that Thanos destroyed the stone, low-tuned during Stark’s funeral and high and exciting when the heroes resurrect. The instruments used for the film include but are not limited to Brass, Strings and Percussion & Mallets. These perfectly match up with the superhero and time travel themes. As well, the sound effects are great. My favorite sound effect is with the Infinity Stones, as they sound haunting, but in an ethereal way. But my favorite aspect is the tracks. They are iconic, perfect volume and always get the scene right. Especially with “Portals” and “Main on End”. **VERDICT: 20/20**

# In conclusion, this film is one of three Marvel Cinematic Universe movies that are so masterful, “too good to be true” is exactly how I would describe them. Avengers 4: Endgame is my personal favorite movie in the Marvel Cinematic Universe. It absolutely stuck the landing, because I wanted to finish the end credits after the movie, despite how long they could be. It is a superhero movie that I would recommend to any superhero fan. **Final Score: 100%**
