# **Kung Fu Panda (2008)**. There is an incredible amount of backstory into how this movie was made. First off, do you remember Po for how kind he is? Well, that almost did not happen. You see, he was originally considered as a lazy, egotistical jerk. But when Jack Black put in innocence into the role, the writers had to change that. Also, to accurately bring the martial art, the animators actually took Kung Fu 101, and the movie was supposed to be 2D Animated but they became 3D. It’s composer Hans Zimmerman had traveled directly to China’s Wudang Mountains to use the China National Symphony Orchestra to compose the score. Now normally, an animator would be punished by being fired if they could not finish a scene, but they were motivated, because whenever they did, they were rewarded by fortune cookies. Lastly, the film took four and a half years to officially release in theaters. I would say that the risk paid off, because the film hit 632 million dollars worldwide, and against a 130 million dollar budget , the film became a box office hit. But is this movie that good or just a big fat project? **SPOILERS!**

# **Story**

# In Kung Fu Panda (2008), Po Ping is a hardworking noodle restaurant worker by his father Mr. Ping. However, when Grandmaster Oogway announces the King Fu tournament happens today, where whoever’s combat skills impresses him the most is whom he deems as “The Dragon Warrior”. When Po accidentally lands in the ring, Oogway gives him the title. Po then trains with Shifu to let him become a legend of Kung Fu, stopping the villainous Tai Lung. First off, yes, the film can have generic roots. Such as anthropomorphic characters, and Po being an underdog in a Kung Fu movie. This happens a lot. However, its execution makes everything different. One: The dragon scroll does not give Po superpowers. It is a blank scroll, but that causes him to believe in himself. Also, I expected Shifu to make Po let go of his eating habit and weight. He makes him use them to his ability. Another thing I thought was actually well handled was the pacing. The film is merely 92 minutes long. However, the movie makes the pacing work, because every scene is paced perfectly, and then it enters the next scene. For instance, when Oogway gives Po advice not to give up, the scene gives us everything we need. Po thinks about giving up, Oogway convinces him not to, and then we cut to Tai Lung escaping prison. Also, there is an appropriate amount of lore. This includes how Master Shifu trained the furious five vs how he trained Tai Lung, the snow leopard’s descent into evil, and how important the dragon warrior convention is. It makes the world feel lived in, without making it feel too crowded with information. My only real gripe is the story structure. The training montage does not start until the 53-minute mark, so it may feel a bit rushed. But what makes me find the story amazing regardless, is the tone. The film gives us slapstick situations, such as Po’s efforts to get into the jade palace, and serious moments, such as the panda meeting his father before the climax. What keeps this film from feeling tonally inconsistent is that it focuses more on its comedy than its heart, so it knows which one to present more. But my favorite aspect of the storytelling of DreamWorks’ 17th animated feature is its themes. The movie teaches you, “If you want to be special, you just have to believe you are special with hard work in the mix”. That teaches people to have self esteem, without being arrogant. **VERDICT: 18/20**

# **Animation**

# We just went from amazing to fantastic. The animation looks so gorgeous, and very close to perfect. The only real downgrade is the color pallet. The film’s main theme is red, and it never looks terrible, but the animators never really did anything with the color. At least the blue represents the coldness of our antagonist. But then, you have everything else that was incredible. First off, the textures. They look very detailed and very accurate to what it would look like in China. The prison, the Sacred Peach Tree Of Heavenly Wisdom, Shifu’s dojo, everything. That, and there are incredible details. The peach tree is made of pink leaves, the prison is in the middle of snow, and the dojo is around grass, yet had wooden floors. The overall story is 3D animated, but the prologue is 2D. That is because of Po’s fantasies being different from what actually happens. My other praise is that the character expressions are very accurate to their key character traits. Po clumsily bounces around the room (Silliness), Shifu’s twitch, yet non-slouching posture (Discipline) and Oogway moving slowly (Patience). But my favorite part is the action. This is far more than bouncing slapstick, but rather very accurate to Tai Chi. The choreography is fluid, the characters fight like their animal style, and the cinematography supports every move. **VERDICT: 19/20**

# **Characters**

# The characters are incredibly well-developed, and they all had to be in this story, or else I would feel like they were missing something. Po is our main protagonist. While I thought a panda’s father being a goose made no sense, the sequel made it make sense. But on a serious note, Po is the Panda who wants to know who the dragon warrior is. But when Oogway appoints him the role, that starts one of DreamWorks’ greatest character arcs. Po becomes better and better at Kung Fu, and starts believing in himself. His positives are that he is very determined, yet kind, and his flaw is that he makes jokes at… not always the best times. He is very good-natured, even if flawed. And I relate a lot to his arc of not believing in himself, because sometimes I find myself in his shoes. Master Shifu is his mentor, and I thought he would be a perfect character from beginning to end. However, he is flawed, and he develops. He is very unreasonably hard on his students, but as he trains Po, he learns to be kinder. When he apologizes to his first student, this shows his character growth. The furious five members are Tigress, Monkey, Viper, Mantis and Crane. The five have a great character arc combined of seeing Po as a hard worker and not just a jokester with no talent. My only problem is that I wish they could set themselves apart from each other. Oogway is Shifu’s mentor, and the catalyst for Po’s character journey, both literally and metaphorically. He recruits him as the dragon warrior, and helps him realize his full potential. His flaw, though, is that his rejection of Tai Lung was the cause for his villain arc. Mr. Ping is Po’s father, and the part that makes him come close to giving up. Pretty bad parenting, right? Not really. He still loves his boy, and encourages him to believe in himself near the end. And then there is my personal favorite villain in all of DreamWorks, Tai Lung. What makes him a tragic villain is that it was not his fault he was rejected. Shifu was the one who planted his “Destined for greatness” mindset in him, which brought him down a dark path. The film explains why Tai Lung is so angry, but never excuses his waste to the Valley Of Peace. You feel bad for him, because you see who he could have been. But he backs that up with actual fear factor he can instill in the audience. **VERDICT: 19/20**

# **Acting**

# Now, we get to perfection. The acting is so good, because every single actor puts in a very noticeable amount of diligence in their performances. Jack Black is Po. Black was perfectly cast as Po, and his comedy factor is living proof. Seriously! Every time Black delivers a funny line, it works so well. But Black nails the serious moments. Dustin Hoffman is Master Shifu. Hoffman is amazing at serious roles, and Shifu is living proof of that. He really gets the sense of discipline that must be put into his character, but is very humorous at the funnier moments. The furious five are portrayed by Angelina Jolie, Jackie Chan, Lucy Lou, Seth Rogen, and David Cross. All five of them put in a lot of effort to set themselves apart, and I would say it works. Randall Duk Kim is Oogway. Duk Kim is very humorous, but sounds very wise and good at taking themselves seriously. Lastly, Ian McShane is Tai Lung. McShane puts a lot of scariness into his character, but you can hear a lot of pain in his silences. **VERDICT: 20/20**

# **Music**

# The music is incredible. It really gets what kind of movie it is a part of, and it sounds incredible. First of all: it has that Chinese vibe. It really sounds like it is about a movie from China, and you even hear a couple of Chinese beats in the mix. Second thing: the action is full of high orchestral swings. The music sounds so powerful. You really feel every musician here is giving it their all. But I must have a favorite track, right? Yes. At number three: "Oogway Ascends" is emotional and the piano tracks are amazing. Number Two: "Tai Lung Escapes" is the best at sounding intense. It starts off building up, as the prisoner breaks out and then it goes off the rails. And number one is: "Hero". It is basically the main theme, and every beat hits hard. **VERDICT: 20/20**

# **In conclusion**, Kung Fu Panda (2008) is an incredible movie. Its negative are very rare, and there are so many positives. I love all five aspects about this movie, and would recommend this to any animation fan. **Final Score: 96%**
