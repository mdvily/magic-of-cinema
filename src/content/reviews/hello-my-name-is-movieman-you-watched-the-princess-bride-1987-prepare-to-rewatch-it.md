# **The Princess Bride (1987)**. Based on the 1973 novel, this movie was acclaimed from the start. In 1987, it received an A+ CinemaScore, and has been added in 2016 to the National Film Registry for being "culturally, historically, or aesthetically significant." IMDb gave it an 8, and a 93% on Rotten Tomatoes. In all honesty, I hardly think of one person who gives the film the term, “Overrated”. Now, this can happen to a film like this, when it survives on legacy alone. But Snow White And The Seven Dwarfs (1937) survived on mostly legacy, but is still acclaimed for quality (regarding animation, acting and the dwarves). But are we going to have fun storming this castle?

# **Story**

# The story has this Story-Within-A-Story structure, where a grandfather reads to his ill grandson. The overall story is incredibly moving. The grandson wanting his grandfather to stay in the end makes me cry every time. This is because the film builds up this affection to the grandfather and the story. Now onto the fantasy. The fantasy element and setting aspect are very creative. The ROUSes, a cynical miracle man and a very campy tone. However, it never underestimates the minds of the audience. You then have the promises. For example, Inigo promises he will kill the six fingered man. The result? The six fingered man dies by him in the end. The pacing is spot on. Every scene moves at a fast yet slow enough pace. This makes the film engaging for its audience, yet never takes too long. Now, it could have felt too fast, but it did not here. Lastly, the romance is incredible. There are actual challenges between Buttercup and Westley, and it makes their relationship feel earned. My only real gripe is that the grandson interrupting the story gets pretty annoying. **VERDICT: 19/20**

# **Visuals**

# The Princess Bride (1987) looks absolutely beautiful. Now, normally, I would review the visuals, but instead, here are six reasons why I find the visuals excellent. One: The movie was filmed at the Cliffs of Moher in Ireland. This makes the movie feel less artificial, and the wide shots of the film give it a grandiose sense of adventure. Two: The settings give the movie a real fairy tale vibe. They would already look beautiful on their own. However, the movie goes the extra mile, in order to make them look appropriate for a fairy tale story. Three: The color palette is exemplary. The movie perfectly utilizes every color of the rainbow, in terms of setting and effects, but what stands out the most is the amount of red. Prime examples are Buttercup’s dress and the autumn leaves. Four: the film’s soundstage aesthetics were great for this film. Some indoor environments—such as the Fire Swamp—were built 100% on studio soundstages. They look very theatrical well into a cozy storybook atmosphere. Five: The special effects. They fit so well into a story like this, and I do not find them dated in the slightest. Six: The action is very enticing. A prime example of this is the battle between Westley and Inigo. The actors move so fluidly without any annoyingly distracting special effects or camera cuts. **VERDICT: 20/20**

# **Characters**

# I honestly find the characters in this movie the weakest of the five aspects. We begin with the only not good, just mediocre character in the movie, Princess Buttercup. The film underutilizes female characters and she is a key example of that. Yes, she learns to fight back against Humperdinck, but she learns it in one scene. That happens way too late, and it stops her character arc from feeling earned. Westley, on the other hand, is so full of life, and I love how his lack of a character arc here is a good thing. It gives the side characters more time to shine. Though, he actually drives the story forward, actually has character traits and actually can fend for himself. Fezzik has the biggest character arc. He goes from being Vizzini’s henchman to a brave hero. This works because even better because he always had a heart of gold. Just a bad way of showing it. Humperdinck is a humorous villain, that never meshes with the tone and is a great foil to Westley. You see, they both want Buttercup. But Westley does because he genuinely loves her. Humperdinck just wants to marry her because that is the expectation and so he can kill her. The grandson and grandfather outside of the book also have wondrous chemistry. The grandson starts out rejecting his grandfather, but learns to not do so. He even begs him to stay in the end. That shows a great and earned change in character. But if you want to talk about The Princess Bride (1987) characters, you cannot miss out on Inigo Montoya. His personality and character traits are clear, he is a very humorous, yet serious character without giving me tonal whiplash and his character arc of fighting against the man who butchered his father works wonders. To conclude, the characters are my least favorite aspect of the movie, but they still work amazingly well. **VERDICT: 18/20**

# **Acting**

# The acting is honestly on another level, because every actor puts in a noticeable amount of passion into their characters. Cary Elwes is Westley. Elwes sounds so charming, and intelligent, and has excellent chemistry with Wright. Robin Wright is Buttercup. While Buttercup was not a great character, I cannot deny the effort Wright puts in to her character’s voice and facial gestures. Mandy Patinkin played Inigo Montoya. Patinkin was so charismatic, even when his character was kind of at his lowest point. But the film presents his portrayal as humorous, yet terrifying, when need be. This makes his performance very multilayered. André the Giant is Fezzik. The Giant is absolutely hilarious, but has such a warm demeanor that meshes perfectly with his character. Chris Sarandon was Humperdinck. Sarandon gives off so many humorous one-liners, but has an actually threatening presence. Fred Savage as the grandson and Peter Falk as the grandfather are last on the list, and they do an excellent job at having great relationship with each other. **VERDICT: 20/20**

# **Music**

# The music here is an absolute bop, with plenty of iconic tracks, even 39 years later. They sound like they are from a fairy tale, but have their own charm, such as "Once Upon a Time...Storybook Love" and "A Happy Ending" are what help the film sound beautiful in a positive way. But if you want it in a negative way, you have "I Will Never Love Again", which sound authentic, yet tearjerking. And then, the action tracks. "The Swordfight", "The Fireswamp and the Rodents of Unusual Size" and "Revenge" sound so enticing and exactly what the film should have. Every music beat is built up to, every track sounds appropriate for this film and Mark Knopfler put in his all to every beat. Excellent work, Mr. Knopfler. But my favorite track is "Storybook Love" by Willy DeVille. DeVille’s song is beautiful and fits perfectly in this movie. While we care more about the quotes, it feels like a crime to let the music go unnoticed. **VERDICT: 20/20**

# **In conclusion**, The Princess Bride (1987) is a timeless classic of the 80’s, and a near-perfect film. With very little shortcomings, it proves to be one of the best movies of 1987. But the real reason we watch this feature is the quotes. You remember the movie when you hear: "Hello. My name is Inigo Montoya. You killed my father. Prepare to die.", "Inconceivable!", "As you wish." And so much more. If you like the book, fantasy or any movie of the 80’s, you need to see this movie. **VERDICT: 97%**
