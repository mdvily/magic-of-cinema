Greetings. I am setting out to review all of the MCU movies before Avengers: Doomsday (2026). For the list, I started out with Iron Man (2008). The second film on the docket is **The Incredible Hulk (2008)**. Right off the bat, the 2003 movie divided audience, so Marvel Studios decided to not tell the same story. Instead, the opening credits only would feature Banner’s past. The script was handled by Zak Penn, but his actor, Edward Norton, made plenty of changes. This was in order to focus more on Banner’s internal conflict. Also, the hulk from five years ago at the time was modeled to be 6 feet larger than this version. This was inspired from An American Werewolf in London during the transformation sequences. Finally, Disney did not collect Marvel at the time, so this was distributed by Universal Pictures.**
Story**
=======

Honestly, the action scenes carry the film. They bring so much excitement to the picture, and make you feel this movie is way more immersive than it is. Because the real story is… awful. It is so bland and dull, with the one other saving grace being that the dark tone is different from the rest of the MCU. First off, the VIP on the run subplot is presented as this generic cat and mouse chase, only the mouse can defend himself. This makes for something very generic with very little substance. The romance also comes off as very generic. Maybe it is because the characters are separate too much, or the actors have poor chemistry, or maybe both. Also, why did the film skip to the present and have the story linear? It makes you come off as confused on this movie. But the worst part is that the comedy is unfunny, and the heart comes off as lacking, because the film does not care. **VERDICT: 6/20
Visuals**
=========

# This film looks really good, but unlike Iron Man (2008), some CGI has aged like milk. While I appreciate the effort to make the characters of Hulk and Abomination look grotesque, whoever is in charge of the CGI goes way too far, because some shots make me feel like the character designs are dandruffing off during the fights. That is a very big problem, because the CGI is what I came for, and I left the movie disappointed. But everything else is great. For one, the characters’ clothing is highly accurate to the characters. Betty has softer clothes to convey her innocence, while her father has a dark green suit to show he means business. The cinematography is sweeping, too, as it makes every environment look huge. Especially the Military Station or the city. My next praise is the color palette. It takes on a theme of dark green to prove how sinister this world is. But for the softer scenes, you get the main shade of light blue. But the action is my least favorite part of the visuals I actually liked. Yes, the choreography needs improvement, but the violence is there and very appropriate. **VERDICT: 17/20**

# **Characters**

# Honestly, the characters are on average, bad. Now, the two villains are good, but I only counted five key characters. General Thaddeus “Thunderbolt” Ross is the film’s secondary antagonist, and he is the most complex. On one hand, he wants the Hulk killed to keep the civilians safe. That sounds like a decent reason why, as the Hulk is very destructive. However, he also can be very hard on his soldiers, which makes sense for a military general, even if he crosses lines. Lastly, his fear of Bruce Banner after the gamma radiation effect is understandable. Who would not be scared of a mean, green, killing machine like him. Emil “The Abomination” Blonsky is the main antagonist, and I have a confession to make: I find him very underrated in terms of writing. Yes, his motivation is dull, as he just wants power. However, most villains have wanted power from the start.  Blonsky gets obsessed with it throughout the movie. But the protagonists honestly suck. Dr. Samuel Sterns is only there to put fear into this world. This makes him a plot device. Betty Ross seems way more complex than what she is. Yes, she is snappy at her father and kind to Bruce, but she could have had a character arc of her being more forgiving to Hulk, but that never happens. Lastly, Hulk as the main protagonist is just a nothing burger. There are no character traits, key flaws or a character arc. He just starts the exact same way he ends, which is a cynical scientist with abusive memories from his father Brian. **VERDICT: 8/20**

# **Acting**

# If you only go to the movies to see how good the main lead does, you may be disappointed. You might think that Mark Ruffalo played the Hulk in this movie, as he did with the rest of the MCU, but that honor goes to Edward Norton here. Norton has no real personality here and his calm performances do not match his unhinged portrayal of the Hulk. It also fails to help that he says his lines and moves so boringly. At least the rest of the cast is good, right? Right. Liv Tyler is Betty Ross. Tyler has such a soothing presence to Dr. Banner, and a real attitude to her father. Speaking of which, the late William Hurt is General Thaddeus “Thunderbolt” Ross. Right off the bat, Hurt acts so commanding and strict on screen.  But you can also see a layer of fear inside of him. Tim Roth is Emil “The Abomination” Blonsky. Roth starts off scared and innocent. Then he builds his way up in terms of evilness. Until the end, where he is consumed by power, and Roth’s voice for Abomination is perfect. Lastly, Tim Blake Nelson is Dr. Samuel Sterns. Blake Nelson has such fear in his portrayal. However, instead of screaming it out, he reflects it onto us with his silence. **VERDICT: 16/20**

# **Music**

# Everyone loves a good score, and this movie delivers that in spades. In fact, this is the greatest aspect of the movie. My only real gripe is that the music can be way too loud. Seriously! It is in fact so loud, that it is louder than my parents lecturing me. This stops me from hearing the dialogue that well, but other than that, the music is amazing. What I love the most is that out of all 38 MCU movies, this one sounds the scariest, and the only one to fully lean into the horror genre. These films just want to sound loud and proud, but this one has an identity. This also makes it fit in well with the horror segments. But whenever there is a softer scene, the film’s music meshes well with it too. The music gets right every single scene and has an identity of its own. **VERDICT: 18/20**

# To conclude, The Incredible Hulk is definitely not as good as Iron Man. And when you watch the two, you can see a clear drop in quality. But this movie is not bad. Sure, the writing is, but the visuals, acting and music actually works. If you like the color green and Hulk in general, you can give this a watch. **Final Score: 65%**
