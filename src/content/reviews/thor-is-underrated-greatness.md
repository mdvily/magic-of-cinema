# Greetings. I am setting out to review all of the MCU movies before Avengers: Doomsday (2026). For the list, I started out with Iron Man (2008). The second film on the docket is **Thor (2011)**. This movie was set as a concept by director Sam Raimi in 1991. However, 20th Century Fox did not understand this project and abandoned it. However, by 2006, Kevin Feige realized Marvel Studios still had the core rights to form the Avengers, putting the movie back on track. In the first draft, Odin never banished Thor to Earth, but rather the middle-ages. But they scrapped that idea to save more money. To look accurate to his character, Hemsworth even dyed his beard and hair blonde. This movie has been a dumped-on movie, and I find that unfair. So here is why I find the first MCU movie of 2011 very underrated.

# **Story**

# Honestly, the story is a real missed potential situation. It is anything but terrible, but it could be even better. First off, what makes the storytelling work is quite the things. But the number one best aspect of the storytelling is the tone. It is a film that takes itself seriously in Asgard, and more comedic on Earth. That makes a lot of sense, and stops it from being tonally inconsistent, because different tones happen at different times. Also, the drama is incredible. Everything that happens hits hard because there has been intense buildup. Also the theme of humility is something you need to learn eventually, and is executed well in this movie. However, I do have criticisms. For one, the story is quite unfunny. The quips are more on Thor’s confusion on how Earth goes, and it is full with stupid puns. Two, the “fish out of water” story is played so hollowly. We get this story of an arrogant character who makes a mistake, gets punished and has to learn how to prove he has learned from his mistake. The film does very little to flesh it out. This means that the story can be very predictable, because it does what you would expect. Lastly, the second Thor arrives on Earth, the story was so formulaic that I wanted it to just end. **VERDICT: 10/20**

# **Visuals**

# The visuals in this film work. For one, the Bifröst looks unique. And by unique, I mean a rainbow bridge what looks to be made of glass. Most MCU movies would just focus on one color, but this film focuses on the entire rainbow. That also means that the film is very colorful. Mostly, though, it just sticks with Orange and Blue, to showcase the majesty and the tone. As well, Asgard’s horizon is made of swirling cosmic gas clouds and distant starscapes to showcase that these characters are gods. Also, the earth locations look great, because they were filmed on location. However, there are two problems. One: the action kind of sucks. The choreography is not even there, just characters throwing each other. The camera suffers from shaky cam and it looks cheesy. Also, some special effects, like Thor’s lightning have aged like milk, and I mean the milk the father sometimes leaves to receive. That, and at least every few seconds, there is an angle shot. It looks strange, and there is no reason for it. **VERDICT: 14/20**

# **Characters**

# The characters are mostly good. Almost all of them are great on Asgard, and and some of them not so much on Midgard. Thor is the main protagonist, and he has a strong character arc of learning to be more humble. His mistakes are from his flaw or arrogance and his character growth is well paced. He starts off as a snobbish, bratty prince who likes fighting but after he accidentally starts a war, his father banishes him for it. Now, he must become better than who he already is. Odin is his father, and the All-Father. He actually has character traits though, and development. He is stern, yet loving. He has a clear arc of learning to love both of his sons the same. Jane Foster is less of Thor’s love interest and more of his on earth companion who helps him return home. She has her own agency, and her loyalty and compassion to Thor is very heartfelt. However, some of the side characters are… not the best. Dr. Eric Selvig is somewhat of a character without a character. Like, there is little to no personality to him. Sif and The Warriors Three do not even belong as main cast members of this movie, and we only had to see them for a couple of scenes. Also, Darcy Lewis is Jane’s assistant. Some of her jokes in 2011 were funny, but nowadays, when she calls Mjolnir, “Meow Meow”, I just roll my eyes. But my favorite character of this movie is easily Loki. What a strong villain. What I love most about Loki is his motivation of killing every frost giant to prove himself to his father. **VERDICT: 16/20**

# **Acting**

# The acting is the first aspect of the five I would give my seal of perfection to. Chris Hemsworth is Thor. While not his first ever role, this was his main role that got him noticed as a star. And for good reason. Hemsworth transforms into his character during the happy moments, the sad moments, the angry moments, everything. He always knows how to speak and move as his character. Tom Hiddleston is Loki. Hiddleston is so charismatic, sure, but he also sells the anger of his character, especially when he yells at his father to tell him why he took him in. Anthony Hopkins is Odin. Hopkins is probably the most acclaimed actor of the MCU, and for good reason. Hopkins perfectly gets his role, and says his lines and moves around with such precision, yet vulnerability. Natalie Portman is Jane Foster. She has her own performance agency, and can have attitude, as well as sincerity. Kat Dennings and Stellan Skarsgård. Both perfectly get their roles, rather it be comedy or science. **VERDICT: 20/20**

# **Music**

# Honestly, this movie might have the second best score of the MCU’s Phase One (2008-2012). Everything is amazing, and totally belongs in this movie. First off, "Walk" by the Foo Fighters plays in this movie and it perfectly shows Thor getting the chance to walk again. The pun is great, and it fits the earth part of the story really well. Speaking of which, what Patrick Doyle tries to do with the music is make it sound like classic cinema. Not only does it do the trick, but it perfectly fits the tone perfectly. The volume is loud enough for me to hear it, but Doyle carefully crafts it to be quiet enough to not make your eardrums bleed (Looking at you, Löded Diper). Lastly, each character has a main theme. For Thor, it be "Thor Kills the Destroyer“, and with the royal family of Asgard, "Sons of Odin". **VERDICT: 20/20**

# **In conclusion**, Thor (2011) is a great movie that was quite the step up from The Incredible Hulk (2008) and Iron Man 2 (2010). With a lot of flaws, it is not as good as Iron Man (2008), or later MCU movies, but still a great film you should check out. If you want a fun movie, this is for you. **Final Score: 80%**
