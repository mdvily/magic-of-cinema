# Greetings. I am setting out to review all of the MCU movies before Avengers: Doomsday (2026). For the list, I started out with Iron Man (2008). The next film on the docket is **Black Panther (2018)**. Could you believe that Chadwick Boseman was not guaranteed to be the role of King T’Challa. That honor was supposed to go to Wesley Snipes (Who would later join the MCU as Blade in Deadpool And Wolverine (2024)). Later, this character would be included on a standalone slot of 10 films, in 2005. Boseman was casted in the film announced in 2014, being cast as T’Challa without an audition. Then, in 2016, after Captain America: Civil War, Ryan Coogler was picked to be the director. Coogler rounded T’Challa’s dual conflict of grieving his father’s death and leading a nation. Production designer Hannah Beachler and the creative team spent eight months researching African cultures—including the Dogon, Maasai, and Basotho peoples, traveling to South Africa, Uganda, Argentina, and South Korea to build Wakanda's visual identity. Principal photography ran for three months, from January to April 2017, primarily in Atlanta, Georgia, and Busan, South Korea, this making it the first Marvel Studios film with a Black director and a Black cast. But what makes this Marvel’s most acclaimed movie? **SPOILERS!**

# **Story**

# How is the storytelling in Black Panther the weakest aspect, if it is great, nonetheless? Is it because the film is just absolutely amazing? Yes, it is. But as a critic, I must do my job to review movies, not just blindly praise them. T’Challa surviving his cousin pushing him off a cliff, as a normal human, is a rushed plot hole, the story can feel reminiscent of The Lion King (1994), and the third act is just good vs. evil, without any meat on the bones. But now, as to why the story is great nonetheless: its faults are one-timers, but the good is consistent throughout the entire story. I, for one, find the humor insanely underrated. I think the funniest joke is when T’Challa gets asked by his sister what he is wearing. I have no idea why, but the stupidity of the comedy is precisely why I laugh at it so much. The conflict is very compelling. It asks if a billion-dollar nation can help their race all around the world, via its resources. T’Challa does not want to, but Killmonger does. This results in some very interesting conflict, and I was confused on whose side to be on. The film as well never underutilizes Wakanda as its setting. It gets into every important detail of it, involving its history, politics and resources. And the film lastly has high stakes and intense sequences. Seriously! I started to wonder if Wakanda would live on, or if the throne would be taken. Every scene is intense when need be, and that, along with other reasons, is why the storytelling is flawed greatness. **VERDICT: 16/20**

# **Visuals**

# Black Panther is an absolutely beautiful film. While it is not as great as other MCU movies, the visuals are excellent. Outside of some writing issues, the only criticism I give the film is that the Computer Generated Imagery (CGI) is awful. It has dull colors, and feels like it dandruffs off of the characters, if you know what that means. Now for everything that makes the visuals amazing, nonetheless. For starters, the costume design. Every single character looks magnificent, and you know which character it is, just by the suit. Black Panther’s is mostly black with some purple, and Killmonger’s is half black, half golden. As for the production design, plenty of the movie is filmed on location. Such as the South Korea casino, or plenty Wakandan areas, which were all filmed in Africa. I now bring you to the action. Each action scene brings to the table an appropriate amount of violence and an even better amount of expertly-framed and memorable choreography. Such as T’Challa vs. M’Baku, the car chase, and most notably the first fight between the cousins. And we end the visual journey with the cinematography. Every shot brings warmth, rich, neon-color palettes and deep skin tones. The lighting and framing brings these three together so well, and it results in Black Panther to look amazing. **VERDICT: 18/20**

# **Characters**

# The cast in this movie is immensely underrated, and I wish them better reception in the future. Let us begin with King T’Challa/Black Panther. T’Challa has a strong and highly compelling character conflict. Does he want to help the black population, or honor the legacy of Wakandan royalty, most notably his father? It is very well-focused and in the end, it feels earned. He slowly finds out his core motivation, and the ending where he uses his kingdom’s resources is very satisfying. Erik “Killmonger” Stevens/N’Jadaka is the villain, and honestly my third favorite MCU villain right now. This is because Killmonger is an extreme threat. When he threw his own cousin off a cliff, I actually believed he did it. But at the same time, while he does horrible antics, you can see where he is coming from. He wants to give Wakandan technology to black people all across the globe, to fix their racism struggle. You understand his motive, but do not agree with it 100%. That is perfect explaining, not excusing. So our two leads are absolutely fantastic. But our other main characters? Absolutely! I mean, Shuri as his sister gets a lot of hate, and I did not think that was well deserved back then. She was a side character in her debut film, and I say she did a great job! Not only is she hilarious, but I find her compassion for her king highly charming. Queen Ramonda is T’Challa’s mother, and she elegantly rules Wakanda before her son ascends to the throne. At the same time, finding out her husband is a murderer, is deeply heartbreaking. M’Baku is a character expected to be a one-dimensional side-villain. But they did something else here. They made him hate T’Challa at the start, but deeply care about his people. He even has a clear character arc or standing for the king, and putting his differences against Black Panther beside him. Yes, that is not comic-book accurate, but since when has that been a valid criticism? Okoye and Nakia are T’Challa’s most loyal subjects, and their loyalty is extremely mature, yet heartwarming. Finally, I am conflicted on Klaue. Yes, he served his purpose as a threat in the first act, but after that, once the CIA arrests him, why not just abandon him? It does not really make sense. But if you can make your way past that, you will be in for an amazing cast. **VERDICT: 19/20**

# **Acting**

# Now, we get to perfection! The late Chadwick Boseman is T’Challa/Black Panther. Boseman brings so much gravitas to T’Challa, and makes him feel human with his accent and every single gesture. On top of that, he perfectly nails the edge between feeling like a leader, and a lost son. Michael B. Jordan is Erik “Killmonger” Stevens/N’Jadaka. Jordan perfectly understands the casual talk for the first half, and for the second, he is absolutely terrifying. Jordan fully transforms into a man broken by loss and shame of his cousin, and blends it perfectly with a fear factor. Letitia Wright is Shuri. Now, as I mentioned, Shuri can be extremely annoying. But I think that Wright’s performance turns some of it into humor. She is simply hilarious in this role. Wright gets every joke, and the line deliveries and facial expressions perfectly capture the scene. Like when she asks, “WHAT ARE THOSE???” to her older brother. Winston Duke is M’Baku. Duke perfectly gets the mixture between a cocky rival and a noble friend. He is less humorous than the likes of Wright, but that means he gets the tone of his character perfectly, because M’Baku is not a funny character. Angela Bassett, Danai Gurira and Lupita Nyong'o are Queen Ramonda, Okoye, and Nakia. I put them together because I get the same feeling from each character. The three perfectly capture regality, and support for T’Challa. Andy Serkis is Klaue. Serkis is both terrifying and humorous at the same time. **VERDICT: 20/20**

# **Music**

# This movie honestly has my favorite music in any MCU movie in the last decade, with everything (Both Ludwig Göransson‘s score) and the album culminating in something incredible. The volume is never too loud, nor too quiet. I heard every single line, every sound effect, every beat. For instance, when T’Challa stabs his cousin, you hear the low brass beat, and the slashing into the chest. Second, the music gets every single scene perfectly. The car chase to get Klaue, the backstory of Wakanda, the declaration of T’Challa turning king, the list goes on. Every scene has appropriate beats and rhythm. Göransson would as well travel to Senegal to study traditional music and record with local musicians, including drum percussionist Massamba Diop and vocalist Baaba Maal. T'Challa's theme is brought to us by energetic talking drums and heroic brass, while the villain Killmonger's theme is anchored by a haunting West African tambin flute with modern trap and 808 hip-hop beats. These work to convey who these characters are. But the music is not brought to masterpiece status by its score, but rather by its soundtrack. "Pray for Me" is a jam to listen to, but "All the Stars" is iconic. It perfectly captures the entire tone of the movie, and is my favorite liscenced song in the entire Marvel Cinematic Universe. **VERDICT: 20/20**

# **In conclusion**, Black Panther (2018) is a fantastic movie. Now, do I think that this had the potential to be the greatest MCU movie of all time? Yes. But is it amazing on its own? Of course! In fact, I view Black Panther as the most different MCU movie, and the one that feels the closest to an indie movie. That is a good thing, because it enhances how the film as the most special. **Final Score: 93%**
