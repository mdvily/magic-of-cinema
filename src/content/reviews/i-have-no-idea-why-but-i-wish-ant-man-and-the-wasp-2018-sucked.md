# Greetings. I am setting out to review all of the MCU movies before Avengers: Doomsday (2026). For the list, I started out with Iron Man (2008). The next film on the docket is Ant-Man And The Wasp (2018). I think we should start off with how Peyton Reed, the film’s director said they wanted to make the movie "weird, unique and different". Why? Because the origin story was already out of the way. As well, before production, the writers wanted to explore Janet Van Dyne’s backstory and introduce well-regarded actress Michelle Pfeiffer to the Marvel Cinematic Universe. And to culminate the post-credits scene, the writers wanted to make the film align with Infinity War. And lastly, the film’s costume designer Andy Park updated the 1960s-inspired suits to modern tech, adding practical wings to the Wasp suit that would transition into digital effects during flight segments. But is this movie as good as the first movie? **SPOILERS!**

**Story**

I must say, I am mixed on the intention. The film does not want to be a masterpiece of the MCU, just a fun sequel. And while the filmmakers do understand the source material, they never really try hard to make it good, so minus points for laziness. As well, the emotional aspect is just stupid. I watched the film, thinking the movie could make me cry like how I unexpectedly did in the first movie. And because of the mean spirited tone, I just felt dead inside. And that did not do me any favors, after Thanos killed half the population. I just sat there, thinking “What are we even doing here?” And another thing is that the character stories of overcoming grief never match up well with Ghost killing to be cured. Never. They just feel like two different movies. And that could have been fixed if Ghost tried to stop them because Janet accidentally ruined her life. It would not be comic book accurate, but it would still be engaging, and the stories would connect. And the last thing I make the choice to criticize the film about is how tonally inconsistent it is. I mean, how the heck are you going to make a story about overcoming grief that hits hard, and then put in a bunch of jokes. The only reason on why the film is not a 0/10, is because the comedy is absolutely hilarious. While comedy is subjective, a lot of the jokes landed, such as the heist Scott Lang is operating with his daughter Cassie is just them bonding when he is under house arrest. The jokes are absolutely ridiculous, but that is why it works. **VERDICT: 4/20**

**Visuals**

The visuals are easily the greatest part of the movie, and the reason the film is on the better side of mediocrity. For five reasons: One: the shrinking factor. Every time a character changes size, the CGI makes full use of it. As a result, it looks seamless. I also love Wasp’s choreography. She features a ballet-like, acrobatic fighting style. This is very unique, which makes of full use of her character. Then, there is de-aging. This in fact, is some of the best in the MCU. We only see it in the flashback with Hank and Hope. But it looks so accurate to who these characters are, and I believed it. And then, you have the quantum realm. The realm is a delight of colors, and considering how it looked in the sequel, the lighting is impressive. Even if unlike Doctor Strange, it could have taken more risks. But the color in general is great! It has vibrant suits, a full-on realm of beauty and well-made flashback scenes. **VERDICT: 19/20**

**Characters**

The characters are just mediocre. They never make me turn off the movie, but do not absolutely bring me in love with this feature. There are six main characters, two good, two mediocre, two bad. Wasp and Ant-Man are the ones I like. Let us start off with Wasp. She is a bullied character in the first by the screenwriters. And I love how in the sequel, they finally get her right! She actually has a purpose to help Scott learn from his mistakes. And at the same time, she learns to let go of her anger for her father and the loss of her mother. And at the same time, Marvel still made it incredibly enjoyable. Ant-Man/Scott Lang is a humorous side character this time around, who still has an interesting character conflict. Will he balance being Cassie’s father and a superhero, without making the same mistakes again? And in the end, his flaws do not just go away. But they are definitely dialed back. That is great character writing, because it feels real. And now, for the characters who are just mediocre. He has a character arc of standing up for what is right, but it feels incredibly confusing and inconsistent. I also find Janet Van Dyne heavily underutilized. Yes, she is hardly in the film, but that did not have to be the case! They could have made a side plot with her, where she tries to return home. It would work because it fits the story. At least it is satisfying when she does return because it has been built up to. Ava “Ghost” Starr is the villain. Why is she such a terrible antagonist? While I do sympathize with her, she has no purpose in the story and her being a twist reveal makes no sense. Why not just let her be a villain? And finally, we have Sonny Burch. Are you kidding me?! Burch is a loser who has no purpose in this franchise. He just wants to steal Dr. Pym’s lab. That has no purpose to the story, and is as ridiculous as it seems. **VERDICT: 10/10**

**Acting**

I mean, the cast is kind of good. Paul Rudd is Scott “Ant-Man” Lang. If you had to describe Paul Rudd as Scott “Ant-Man” Lang, how would you in one word? For me it would be charming. Rudd has this charisma and vibe that I cannot describe. All I can say is that it feels real, because he also sells the serious moments. Michael Peña is Luis. Peña is a real fast talker here, and that works very well within his character. Also, he does an amazing job at just being humorous. Evangeline Lilly is Hope “Wasp” Van Dyne. Lilly portrays Wasp with a real edge, having strong chemistry with Rudd and Douglas. But this time, Lilly gets into the emotion even better then she did back in the first movie. Michael Douglas is Dr. Hank Pym. Remember his emotional performance in the first? Gone. Instead, all there is, is just an angry portrayal with little range. Hannah John-Kamen is Ghost. Did she even try with this acting career? John-Kamen is incredibly tedious in this role and her voice and body movements put me to sleep first time. Not a bad actor, but maybe this role was not meant for her. Michelle Pfeiffer is Janet Van Dyne. Finally, some good performances, again! While we barely see Janet, Pfeiffer does put in a lot of emotion, especially when she hugs her daughter. Walton Goggins is Sonny Burch. I have no idea what the heck they were doing to cast him outside of Tarantino, but he gave a fantastic performance as this slimy weasel. In fact, the sliminess is key to make Burch feel real. And Goggins nails it! **VERDICT: 14/20**

**Music**

I would consider the music pretty good, because there are things that do tend to hold it back, but it definitely does more good than harm. Sometimes, the music can be too quiet. For instance, during Starr’s backstory, I heard no music, only dialogue. So Christophe Beck could have made the music louder. And at the same time, the heroes’ themes could have blended together more. But get past that, and there is a lot to like. It starts with this: Beck introduces a bold, brass-heavy motif for Hope van Dyne in the starting track, "It Ain't Over Till the Wasp Lady Stings," which carries a lot of the sequel's musical weight. The antagonist Ghost gets an intense, haunting theme heavily utilizing ominous strings, vocals, and choir layers on tracks like "Ghost = Toast". Lastly, that iconic, irregular-beat Ant-Man theme makes multiple brief cameos, while synth-heavy tracks like "I Shrink, Therefore I Am" and "Windshield Wipeout" drive the action sequences. **VERDICT: 15/20**

**In conclusion**, Ant-Man And The Wasp (2018) is just your average movie. While it did have a haunting post credits scene, it suffers from an unfocused tone, poor heart and a horrible villain. However, the music, acting and visuals do help the film to be at least tolerable. If you like the first, then you can check this out, just lower your expectations. **Final Score: 62%**
