# Greetings. I am setting out to review all of the MCU movies before Avengers: Doomsday (2026). For the list, I started out with Iron Man (2008). The next film on the docket is **Avengers 3: Infinity War (2018)**. It all started when Joss Whedon, who directed the first two Avengers movies decided not to return. And in 2016, the MCU revealed phase three was revealed. Infinity War would originally be Avengers: Infinity War Part One of Two. The second part would be a different, standalone story, before it was Avengers: Endgame (2019). Photography began in January 2017 at Atlanta Studios in Georgia, and it was shot back-to-back with its sequel. It was as well one of the first ever movies shot with Arri Alexa IMAX digital cameras. Now, you may be asking, “How did they prevent fake leaks?” Two words: Fake. Scripts. They wrote fake screenplays to avoid getting the movie leaked before release. Production completed with a heavy budget of $345,000,000. 

# **Story**

# The story is widely condsidered the most depressing of the MCU’s 19 movies at the time, but a reason is the comedy. Yes, the film is tonally inconsistent, but it makes it work. This is because the comedy brings us to the feeling everything will be all right, and when it is not, it hits even harder, because it is unexpected. And the jokes themselves are hilarious! You have “Why is Gamora?”, a Footloose (1984) reference and Hulk’s refusal to come out. Every joke gets a laugh out of me, and I laugh harder and harder when I understand what they mean. But the more dramatic element is not dialed back. These scenes include Gamora’s death, Loki getting choked, and Peter Quill losing his marbles, finding out his girlfriend is deceased. I actually cried during these scenes, either because I felt bad for the characters, or because it was built up to. But the movie does not just have good storytelling in terms of its tone, but as well its intentions. The writers and Fiege wanted this to be a 10-year-anniversary tribute to Marvel, so they worked hard. But my favorite part of the storytelling is the ending. It works, because there was an opportunity to defeat Thanos, but because of their flaws, the Avengers ruined it. That, and this was the first ever Avengers movie to not have a happy ending. Thanos actually succeeded in killing half the population. And finally, the writers Christopher Markus and Stephen McFeely knew exactly who to kill off, and who to do it in front of, which hurts you even more. **VERDICT: 20/20**

# **Visuals**

# Oh my goodness, this is one of the finest-looking films of the entire MCU. And part of that is because the CGI is a massive improvement over Black Panther, which was released next to two months before the film. And by the CGI, I mean what each infinity stone can do and the chitauri in the final battle. Each stone possesses the power to be an absolute treat to the eyes, and the chitauri look as immensely detailed as they did when we saw them beforehand. That is another thing that the movie does well: attention to detail. Such as a cube-shaped blue tesseract that causes a tiny explosion when Thanos receives the Space Stone, the straps on his chin and the magic of Doctor Strange and Wong. All of that has an extreme focus, and the fact that it took so long to make is believable. The suits are also incredibly well-made, and full of color that makes the lighthearted tone on some of them believable. I think this is most appropriate with the Guardians, and Spider-Man. Speaking of which, the lighting perfectly captures the mood of every scene, especially during Loki’s death. And the colors can still be vibrant, appropriate for an intergalactic experience. They did not just make six infinity stones, calling it a day, but rather made us see what they can do, and humbly present us with many vibrant shots. But the final praise is the action choreography. Each fighting scene is so vibrantly shot, such as the team vs Ebony and Cull, Natasha, Captain America, Wanda and Vision vs. Proxima and Corvus, and especially the final battle. **VERDICT: 20/20**

# **Characters**

# The characters are nothing short of wonderful, but at the end, we will get to the greatest character of the bunch. I think that the heroes are all consistently phenomenal, and there are 19 of them. Steve “Captain America” Rogers is the leader of the Avengers. Rogers has been very torn down by the events of Civil War, where the Avengers fell apart, but I still love how he tries to fight for what he believes is right. That is a true leader. Not only is that heavily inspiring, but it feels true to who he really is. Tony “Iron Man” Stark is the other main protagonist.  Tony wants Thanos stopped, because of how Thanos traumatized Stark for 6 years. This makes it personal. Thor is the most broken character in the movie, so when he learns to finally fight back against Thanos, it feels satisfying. But at the same time, we feel for him. I mean, who would not be extremely broken, after the loss of their entire family? Bruce Banner is a humorous character, but there is a catch: he cannot turn into the Hulk, this time. This elevates the stakes even more. Black Widow has no character arc, but in a movie like this, she does not need one. She must at least contribute to the story and she does. Especially when she defends Wanda from Proxima. The Guardians of The Galaxy are back, and they are hilarious, but each have meaningful moments. Peter Quill is so worried for Gamora, that when she finds out she was killed by her own father, it is heartbreaking for him. Yes, he acted out, impulsively, but it makes sense for him to do it because he is known for doing this. I mean, when he found out Ego killed his mother, he blasted him across the room. Gamora is Thanos’ daughter, so she has the most personal connection to him. When she kills him, it is heartbreaking for her with a side of relief. Drax is conflicted on if he wants to kill or simply defeat Thanos. He makes the final choice to defeat Thanos, and does his part. So does Mantis by containing him from receiving the final stone. Groot has an actual character arc, though. He goes from being a teenage kid who disrespects his elders, to choosing to be a guardian. Rocket has another character conflict: does he want to honor Yondu’s memory by being a kinder individual, or does he want to be himself? Back to the earth superheroes, Doctor Stephen Strange finally gets a strong character arc. He learns to accept teamwork with the Avengers, and at the same time, he is extremely humorous. Black Panther is very important to the story, because the third act is set in his kingdom. And his conflict is being either Wakanda’s king or an Avenger. Peter “Spider-Man” Parker is your average noble teenager, but that makes it work. It causes him to be believable, but charming as ell. And did I cry when the time imbalance killed him off? Or course I was! Vision is the character needed to survive the most. This is because if he dies, Thanos comes closer to winning. And the ending where his girlfriend has to kill him hurts. Wanda Maximoff is the girlfriend, and the most powerful character of the bunch. But at the same time, you feel bad for her. I mean, come on! She lost her parents, brother, and now boyfriend? Who would not be hurt? The black order/ Thanos’ children are made up of Ebony Maw, Proxima Midknight, Cull Obsisian and Corvus Glave. What I love about them is how competent they are. They actually serve a great threat against the protagonists! But the greatest character in the movie and Marvel Cinematic Universe lineup so far for me is Thanos. Thanos is a competent beast who kills a god, annihilates Hulk and actually succeeds in killing half the population. But he actually has a good reason. Because resources are running out for people to live, and he thinks he has no choice. As for the reason why he did not just double the resources? The writers wanted to explain, not excuse his actions. **VERDICT: 20/20**

# **Acting**

# Every single character puts in their all for every single character in the movie. Like these were the characters that they were made to portray. In fact, many of them are acclaimed for these roles. All of them know when to deliver lines seriously, dryly, cheekily or silently. For the comedic actors, you have Benedict Cumberbatch as Stephen Strange, Robert DowneyJr. as Tony Stark or Chris Pratt as Peter Quill. But for the more serious and emotional actors, you have Zoe Saldaña as Gamora and Tom Holland as Spider-Man. And especially Elizabeth Olsen as Wanda in the third act. And the defiant or noble actors are Chris Evans as Captain America, Scarlett Johansson as Black Widow or the late Chadwick Boseman as Black Panther. And lastly, Josh Brolin as Thanos is my favorite actor of them all in this movie. Brolin perfectly captures the sympathy and fear factor of his character in a recognizable, raspy voice. **VERDICT: 20/20**

# **Music**

# The music is some of the most slept on in the entire MCU. In fact, I start to imagine a world where the score is as acclaimed as the villain. It perfectly understands every single character. For the superheroes, the music is loud and proud, but it is dark with some sympathy with Thanos. This is because of who the characters are, so points for understanding. As well, the Avengers theme is back with Silvestri using heavy horns, driving strings, and full orchestras to match the scale of a massive intergalactic war. That, and not only do "Help Arrives" and "Forge" bring back the Avengers theme, but they bring new life into it. And because the movie is more heartbreaking, it must have emotional and dark tracks, right? Right. It absolutely does, and I think the biggest example is “Even For You” which builds from quiet grief into a massive, tragic orchestral climax. But do you want motifs for the characters in this kind of movie? Rest assured, because there are plenty! These include the original Captain America theme, the core Avengers theme, Ludwig Göransson’s Black Panther theme when the heroes arrive in Wakanda, and “Rubberband Man” by The Spinners, when we first see the guardians. **VERDICT: 20/20**

# **In conclusion**, Avengers 3: Infinity War is the greatest MCU movie of its time. I find that everyone worked so hard to make this one of the best superhero movies yet. If you have watched the MCU, other than this, please give it a shot. **Final Score: 100%**
