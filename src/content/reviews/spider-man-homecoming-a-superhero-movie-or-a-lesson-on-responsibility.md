# Greetings. I am setting out to review all of the MCU movies before Avengers: Doomsday (2026). For the list, I started out with Iron Man (2008). The next film on the docket is **Spider-Man: Homecoming (2017)**. Would you ever believe how dark this movie was supposed to be? But first, why does this movie exist? As in, what was its birthplace? Well, it actually came from how poorly the last Spider-Man film was received by critics. The Amazing Spider-Man 2 (2014) is widely considered Spider’s weakest movie yet. So Sony and Marvel shook hands to create this movie. Sony contained creative control, marketing, and distribution, while Kevin Feige of Marvel Studios produced the film to fully integrate Spider-Man into the Marvel Cinematic Universe. Originally, Peter was supposed to be a YouTuber with a motive to get views, not impress Stark. Speaking of Stark, Nick Fury was supposed to be Peter’s mentor. They almost made him harsh and unyielding. And finally, Peter’s actor Tom Holland secretly attended classes at Bronx High School of Science in New York City to prepare for being a tech-school student. But did this and a $175,000,000 budget pay off? **SPOILERS!**

# **Story**

# You guys saw the title. This film is a lesson on responsibility. Across the movie, Peter slowly learns to realize that he does not need cool powers, but instead the good morals and responsibility that an Avenger would have. That is a very mature and different theme from most of the MCU. It presents Spidey as a different protagonist than the rest of the protagonists. Another thing I love is the tone. Jokes are absolutely everywhere, like animals at a zoo. And every single joke is hilarious. Especially when Peter thinks Tony is hugging him, or the "Iron Man! What are you doing robbing a bank? You're a billionaire." one-liner. But there is such heart, especially with Spider-Man and Iron-Man, or Peter, MJ and Ned. The emotion feels realistic, because they went the extra mile to make the witty banter feel welcome in this movie. As well, the pacing is surprisingly great! Act I is about Peter’s struggles of school, Act II focuses on Peter’s mistakes and Act III is with The Vulture. These work very well, because each act feels like either a buildup or an evolution. My only criticism is the final battle. It just looks like a CGI fest, and the character beats are held back. **VERDICT: 19/20**

# **Visuals**

# Oh, my god! This movie looks ugly as heck! Now, I might as well start with a couple of things I actually liked about the movie’s look. The lenses are expressive and the locations, such as Midtown School of Science and Technology, are at least slightly memorable. And lastly, the occurring theme of green does represent something. That is the sinister nature of the villain. But everything else sucks! One: Jon Watts’ direction is incredibly sauceless. There are no decent shots made whatsoever. The best one is Peter taking his suit off near the ending, but even that looks stupid. The action scenes are just lame. It is always Spider-Man trying to defeat Adrian Toomes in what is clearly a CGI suit. In fact, why did they have to use CGI, and not just hand make one? Even if they did, that is not what it looks like. The color palette is muted and ugly. This does not even come close to matching the energetic tone of this movie. They mostly look like dull shades of black with a little bit of green. That is really it. So while the only thing I disliked about the movie is this, the visuals are still horrible. **VERDICT: 6/20**

# **Characters**

# The characters are such an improvement over the visuals, and unlike the directing, I have very little to complain about. Our main protagonist is Peter “Spider-Man” Parker. Peter is a different protagonist than what we usually get from the MCU. Peter already has his superpowers, but while he is a great kid at heart, he can be very irresponsible. Yes his character arc can feel unearned, as instead of progressively improving, he stops during the third act out of the random, he still is a charming and enjoyable character. MJ and Ned are his ride or dies. They are so enjoyable with their support with Pete, and have a very realistic banter with him. They have fun together, they get made at each other, they consistently have each other’s backs. Tony “Iron Man” Stark is Peter’s mentor. Stark still has his enjoyable factor, but at the same time, his criticism to Young Mr. Parker helps with the tone. It grounds it and makes it surprisingly hit hard. Aunt May is Peter’s legal guardian, and is so endearing. Especially, when they have their chat when she pleads for her nephew to open up to her. Liz is Spider-Man’s love interest. Honestly, she is both charming and sincere to Peter. Lastly, Adrian Toomes/Vulture is the villain. Vulture is a street-level antagonist, and he works because of it. It makes him feel real and his motive to get revenge on Stark for ruining his business makes sense, because Stark’s flaws would absolutely cause Vulture’s house to be on the line. **VERDICT: 19/20**

# **Acting**

# Tom Holland is Peter “Spider-Man” Parker. Holland perfectly captures this upbeat energy. He is such a fun character with the funniest of facial expressions. But during the scene where he gets his suit confiscated, he actually gets emotional. I find this a great example of Tom Holland having range. Zendaya and Jacob Batalon are MJ Watson and Ned Leeds. The two are the definition of enjoyable and have excellent chemistry with Holland. At the same time, they have their falling outs, and it feels real. Robert Downey Jr. is Tony “Iron Man” Stark. His tone in speaking, his body movements, his eye movement, everything is done so well with this character. Marissa Tomei is Aunt May. Tomei is so maternal-ish in her tone and has excellent range. She can love Peter, she can be disappointed in her nephew. Finally, Michael Keaton is the Vulture. Keaton is terrifying in this role, but sounds like an everyday man. I guess that is Michael Keaton for you. He always does what others do but puts enough of his own energy into it. **VERDICT: 20/20**

# **Music**

# Michael Giacchino is a musical mastermind, and that is no difference here. First off, the music’s volume is tangible. Not too loud nor too quiet. That means that the music has an appropriate mixture. What starts the film off is the homage of the 1960s, which to me truly is a “giddy grin that sets a fun tone”. And so speak of the “fun tone”, we have the main Spidey-theme. It is upbeat and soaring, using nimble strings, heavy brass, and a driving rhythm. This perfectly reflects upon The Young Parker’s youthful heroics. And at the same time, it is very enjoyable and energetic, just like our main character. But on the flip side, you have Vulture’s low-horn melody. It is creepy, sure, but so it the character it is designated to, so it just helps out with the tone even more. **VERDICT: 20/20**

# **In conclusion**, Spider-Man: Homecoming is my favorite of the Jon Watts trilogy. It is heavily flawed, but it leans into the John Hughes high school movies and it works. I would recommend this to any Spider-Man or MCU fan. **Final Score: 84%**
