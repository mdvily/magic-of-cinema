# Greetings. I am setting out to review all of the MCU movies before Avengers: Doomsday (2026). For the list, I started out with Iron Man (2008). The next film on the docket is **The Avengers (2012)**. It should come as no surprise that Marvel would gamble its own properties to finance its standalone films (Iron Man, The Incredible Hulk, Thor, and Captain America: The First Avenger) that would ultimately lead up to the massive crossover event of a film. The director was going to be Jon Favreau and the writer as Zak Penn. However, due to a budget friction of Iron Man 2 (2010), Favreau stepped down and gave the director position to Joss Whedon. Whedon would focus on the unity of the six protagonists. Also, the MCU would hire actors such as Scarlett Johansson and Jeremy Renner as Black Widow and Hawkeye respectively, and Mark Ruffalo would replace Edward Norton as Hulk. Lastly, photography kicked off in April 2011 in Albuquerque, New Mexico, before moving to Cleveland, Ohio, and later New York City. But now that The Avengers (2012) is officially the end of Phase One, is this as triumphant as it set out to be, or it it just unwilling to make the sacrifice play? **SPOILERS!**

# **Story**

# The story is my personal favorite in all of Phase One of the Marvel Cinematic Universe. The pacing and story structure is very underrated for sure. Yes, the film takes quite long to establish the characters and let the storytelling take place. But that is warranted because it spends its time doing something completely necessary for a team-up movie: introducing the team members. The other criticism I disagree with is that the comedy is everywhere. First off, it is actually funny. Especially Hulk treating Loki like his destruction machine, that gets me every time. Even the smaller quips get laughs out of me, such as, “Doth mother know you Warren her drapes?”. Also, it serves as a necessary break from all of the high stakes of humanity potentially going extinct. So it works in the film’s favor. Speaking of which, now onto the stuff we all love about this movie. One: the stakes are incredibly high, and this caused me to care about what happens in the movie. We spend plenty of time with the human characters. S.H.I.E.L.D.’s agents, Pepper Potts, and the kid who sees Banner before Natasha enters are given enough time for you to feel the tension. We also get tiny details of the heart of the story. Examples are the protagonists finally working together to save the world and Tony’s undying loyalty to Pepper.

# **Visuals**

# Every single shot of the film in my opinion is executed to perfection, and there are many reasons as to why. First off, I highly criticized the Hulk’s shredded design in The Incredible Hulk (2008). Fortunately, he had a more bulkier design that makes you know that he is one of the protagonists. That is a key example of improvement. Also, the colors are beautiful and way less gray and muted than the later movies. It looks vibrant and easy to look at. The lighting takes on every scene with important shots, such as the rotating of the Avengers in a circle. The scene is shot in light to take advantage of the theme of hope. Also, the CGI is timeless. The chitauri, leviathans and Stark Tower were made out of Computer-Generated Imagery, and it looks timeless. However, you have no necessity to worry, because some parts of Iron Man’s armor and Loki’s scepter were practical. But my favorite aspect is the action scenes. Yes, there are only four, but that is totally okay, because all of them were perfect. The opening scene where Loki enters S.H.I.E.L.D.’s facility is enticing, and perfectly sets the stakes. Stuttgart, Germany is the adapting patriot and the arrogant inventor against the god of mischief. The character beats, such as Captain America saving someone else’s life from the scepter’s blast, resulting in Loki getting arrested. The Helicarrier’s Hulk attack is trauma-building scary, and makes sense for Hulk to be angry. But the battle of New York City is the clear highlight. And oh my gosh, the rotating shot of the six avengers in a circle looks seamless today. **VERDICT: 20/20**

# **Characters**

# The characters honestly are my least favorite aspect of this movie. However, that is the only gripe I have, and on average, I still find the character writing fantastic. They are complex and easy to enjoy. The film first gifts us with six protagonists, so it is logical for us to go through them one by one. Starting with Tony “Iron Man” Stark. On one hand, it is very easy for you to laugh at his jokes, and enjoy his arrogance. However, fans and critics alike also leave out how he had an awesome character arc of putting others before himself. This is most evident when he sacrifices himself by putting the misleading into outer space. Steve “Captain America” Rogers is the second protagonist to be talked about, and he works very well. His annoyance with Stark makes for some humorous comedy. However, he also grows by learning to accept that the world has changed. Thor is up next. Thor is a more serious character than the two, and it makes you care about him. You want him to realize that his brother is beyond saving, and throughout the process of the story, he does. Are we going to discuss Bruce “The Hulk” Banner? Yes, we are. And honestly, what an improvement from the last movie he was in. Last movie, he was a one-dimensional character. He becomes more complex, as yes, he still forces himself into hiding, but his appeal to Black Widow results this season turtle into coming out of his shell. Black Widow is our penultimate protagonist, and she has another strong character arc. She learns that working in S.H.I.E.L.D. is not what she is confined to, as she can still have family outside of it. Also, she is very charming and smart, which works well for the movie. The final protagonist is Hawkeye. He is my only real aspect of the movie I believe aged poorly. Yes, he plays a pivotal role in defeating Loki, but aside from that, he has no real agency. He is just a one-dimensional lackey. Nick Fury is the man who assembled the six of them, and he is more than just a cool character/director of the S.H.I.E.L.D. organization. He has a clear character conflict: he wants to keep the world safe, but he can’t make rash decisions, such as manipulating Captain Rogers into joining him. Loki is the main antagonist, and he is one of the greatest of that universe. He wants more than just power, he wants his father’s approval. However, his actions of mass murder are more than unforgivable. This makes the film explain, but not excuse his actions. At the same time, he is hilarious. **VERDICT: 19/20**

# **Acting**

# The acting is my favorite aspect of the movie which is quite the bar to reach. Robert Downey Jr. is Tony “Iron Man” Stark. Downey Jr. perfectly has a balance in his character. His voice has an arrogant tone, yet his body movements have a sensitivity. That… is an attention to detail. Chris Evans is Steve “Captain America” Rogers. Evans has that patriotism he had in the last MCU movie, but now he has a tone to stark, and a confusion in his eyes about the modern world. Chris Hemsworth is Thor. Hemsworth has a great comedic factor, brought into his character, but his concern for his brother balances it out well. Mark Ruffalo is Bruce “Hulk” Banner. Ruffalo captures the intelligence of The Hulk so well, but at the same time, he has you covered in the fear department. Scarlett Johansson is Natasha “Black Widow” Romanoff. Johansson perfectly enhances the concern, dedication, and compassion of her character very well. That might be hard to do, but she does it in spades. Jeremy Renner is Clint “Hawkeye” Barton. Yes, the character in inconsistent here, but Rennner makes his performance work. He does this mostly with silence and letting the body movements talk. Samuel L. Jackson is Nicholas “Nick” Fury. Jackson perfectly captures Fury’s dedication to keeping the world safe. And at the same time, he nails the monologues, mostly notably at the end to the council. Tom Hiddleston is Loki. Hiddleston has still got it in this movie. On one hand, he nails how scary his character must be. Sometimes, like when he commands the German citizens to kneel, he shouts. But when he gets escorted to his cell, he lets the silence speak for itself. However, my favorite part of his performance is the comedy. Especially when he whimpers. **VERDICT: 20/20**

# **Music**

# If I am being honest, the music is the single definition of incredible. First off, "Live to Rise" is in the end credits scene. This works because it hits home narratively in the film’s favor. The reason is that the song’s title perfectly matches what happens and who these characters are. The film also brings back motifs from the previous movies, normally the patriotism of Captain America: The First Avenger (2011) and the rock themes of Iron Man (2008). Every main character has a theme that can be summed up in one word. For four examples, Tony “Iron Man” Stark: Rock. Steve “Captain America” Rogers: Patriotism. Hulk: Horror. Thor: Energy. This makes you know why the characters are here and their purpose to the movie. The music sounds loud and proud. This matches the tone extremely well, and sounds like everything the MCU has been building up to at this point. But my favorite part is the sheer iconicity. “The Avengers” as the main theme is so memorable, I say it every day. When I watched the film in 2025 with my father and younger brother, I consistently hummed the track every single day. **VERDICT: 20/20**

# **In conclusion**, The Avengers (2012) is a modern classic and groundbreaking. It was the first MCU movie to receive an A+ CinemaScore for a reason. It is a near-perfect movie with an amazing script with wonderful visuals, acting and music. If you love superhero movies and are above the age of 12, you might love this movie. **Final Score: 99%**
