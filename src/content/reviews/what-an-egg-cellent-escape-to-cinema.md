# **Chicken Run (2000)**. Based on the 1963 film The Great Escape, Nick Park pitched the idea of a parody about chickens to Steven Spielberg. Spielberg had once had hundreds of chickens, and loved the movie, so he accepted this idea. The film hired 150 staff members, and in 1997, DreamWorks was hired to be a distributor and a co-financier. The film also became the first feature-length movie to be animated in claymation. This is a big deal for animation, because there have been many more movies to take onto its place. The movie has a rotten tomatoes score of 97%, one of the highest of DreamWorks or Aardman. But is this movie as eggcellent as they say it is? **SPOILERS!**

# **Story**

# Something that makes the Wallace and Gromit shorts work, is the action, suspense and comedy that works very well. These factors were put to the test to make a movie like this, and it succeeded. Something that I love about the film is the worldbuilding, and creativity. These factors movie is allowed to be as creative as possible. This is because of the attention to detail on what happens to these innocent chickens, and what happens when they step out of line. Another praise is how high the stakes are. Eventually, the farm has a pie-making machine, and the chickens are in a dire situation, just for living there. But what makes this more intense is that these chickens did nothing wrong. It makes you hate Tweety even more, and want the chickens to escape. The pacing is great and rightfully slow, and the comedy is humorous. The jokes are mostly one liners, and they are actually good puns. However, I do have my gripes. One: The story is very predictable. Of course Rocky cannot fly, and of course Mrs. Tweety goes unhinged in the third act. I also think that the heart is very lazy. But get past that, you leave yourself with an enjoyable time. **VERDICT: 14/20**

# **Animation**

# If there is one thing I love about Aardman, it must be that on the surface, it looks cute and simple, but deep down, there is an amount of effort put in. I always got the feeling that since the film had big shoes to fill, it had to give it its all. And the studio did that wonderfully. In order to express themselves, the project has round eyes and a large mouth for the characters. That, and the character animation is also amazing. They look fluid, and perfectly convey feelings both in the comedic and serious scenes. Every character looks very different from each other, and full of life. But more impressive? The backgrounds and color palette. The movie looks grey, and that perfectly affects its tone. But the movie is intelligent enough to put in set design spectacle for a lot of scenes. The props are amazing, especially in the climax. The action, though, is the greatest aspect of its look. It is incredibly action packed, and fluid. **VERDICT: 20/20**

# **Characters**

# A rule of prison escape movies is: the viewer must be enticed by the prisoners. Fortunately, that is the case here. The characters are all so likable, that they could all steal the show and be your favorite. However, they can be very one dimensional. Yes, they make the most of their traits, but I expect at least most of the characters to be more than only one character trait. Our protagonist Ginger is passionate for a better life, almost to a fault. You see, she takes things too seriously for herself, she can forget to do this for the others. The next prominent poultry is Rocky Rhodes. Rhodes has the biggest character arc, as he goes from just being there for the chickens, to be popular, to genuinely wanting to help them. The villain of the farm is Mrs. Tweety. She wants to behead and feed to others the chickens, in order to receive better profits. While she can be humorous, my favorite aspect of her is how dark she is as a villain. The other chickens, Babs, Mac and Fowler are Ginger’s friends, and they help bring light into the movie. They help with the tone, but they are way too one dimensional for me to like them. You then have Nick and Fetcher, who help the chickens. I dislike them because the film presents them as criminals, but they are clearly on the good side. Still, the characters are on average, pretty good. **VERDICT: 15/20**

# **Acting**

# Julia Sawalha is Ginger. Sawalha is determined as Ginger, and makes her sound very passionate and creative. Mel Gibson is Rocky Rhodes. Gibson is eccentric and full of life as Rhodes, and really, this might be my favorite animated role of his. Jane Horrocks as Babs, Imelda Staunton as Bunty, Lynn Ferguson as Mac and Benjamin Whitrow as Fowler are the four other chickens. What I love is that the chickens have different personalities, and the actors support that. This makes the world feel lived in. Miranda Richardson is Mrs. Tweety. Richardson sounds unhinged and insane, and that is said with praise. **VERDICT: 20/20**

# **Music**

# Chicken Run is a movie that sounds excellent for three reasons. One: The main theme is excellent. It has a pompous, military style brass march. Yes, it takes itself very seriously, but that is a very good thing, here. It sounds very charming, and has a real tune to it. Two: Whistling, kazoos, and ukuleles to give the chickens a plucky, humorous, and British personality. For some reason, I feel like this is what had to be done for this movie, and it sounds like a chicken movie. Three: Epic action music is very prominent here. "Building the Crate" and "Into the Pie Machine" sound very intense, and treats the situation seriously. **VERDICT: 20/20**

# **In conclusion**, Chicken Run (2000) is an incredibly fun and delightful debut movie of Aardman studios. Yes, there are some writing issues, but the script is solid, and the aesthetic is perfect. I would recommend this to any fan of chickens or escape movies, but even if you are not one of them, you might want to give it a try. **Final Score: 89%**
