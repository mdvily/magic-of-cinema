# Greetings. I am setting out to review all of the MCU movies before Avengers: Doomsday (2026). For the list, I start with **Iron Man (2008)**. In 2008, Marvel Studios was in a financial crisis. This is because Iron Man was not as big of a superhero brand as Spider-Man or the X-Men. But the most important part was that Robert Downey Jr. almost did not get the role of Iron Man. You see, the role could have gone to someone like Tom Cruise or Nicholas Cage. But after this, the movie scored $585.8 million against on average a $145 million budget, making it a hit. It did so well that it started the franchise we refer to as the Marvel Cinematic Universe (MCU). But after this review, we will see if Jon Favreau succeeded with flying colors to start a masterclass in cinema, or does the movie itself deserve to be captured by the Ten Rings. There will be spoilers.

# **Story**

# Tony Stark is a businessman who sells top-tier weapons to the military. After the death of his parents, Stark becomes arrogant and uses jokes to hide behind his trauma. Until when his military friends die, and he gets captured for three months in Afghanistan. In order to escape, Tony has to put his selfishness aside and become a true defender to the public. Now, normally, a film is focused on expanding its universe. Iron Man has no desire to focus on world building, as it instead wants to be a character study.* *And it does a great job at that! The movie perfectly captures who Tony Stark is as a person. He is arrogant and snarky, but has a good heart deep down. One thing I love about the story is that not everything goes perfectly the first time, as Stark struggles a lot. You can really feel how it will take actual effort for Tony to eventually get it right. This is extremely accurate to the world of science. I must factor in as well, how the movie’s story structure is easy to understand. Act I: The setting is in the caves, and Tony, in order to escape, builds the Mark I. Act II: Tony Stark builds the Iron Man suit, and actually fails. But through effort, his successes feel earned. The stakes are not as high as what comes later in the universe, but they do feel more real. We witness political issues, such as industrialism, terrorism and the Middle East war. They feel real, and the execution is Iron Man using his weapons for the greater good. Lastly, the film balances comedy and drama very well But as a critic, I have to do my job and say that the movie is not perfect. We still bump into predictable story beats now and then, such as Obadiah Stane being a villain. However, that does not take away too much from a fantastic movie. **VERDICT: 19/20**

# **Visuals**

# This film looks incredible. First off, the background settings are amazing. They look gritty, palpable, and iconic. An example is with the caves. They look dark, which complement the tone of the scene effectively well. Second, the main color themes, most notably are red and gold. The gold represents Tony’s good traits within, and the red is his arrogance. They perfectly mesh together to create a beautiful suit. Third is the realism of mechanism. The designers of the suits focused on every individual flap, micro-thruster, and joint actuating, which is great. The reason is because many movies would just use CGI. Here, they did not do that. They made an actual suit, and it stands the test of time, even over 18 years later. The weight of the suit is extremely accurate. It feels as heavy as it would be in real life. Finally, the action segments are something to behold. The CGI goes a long way to support the scenes. My favorites Tony’s fight against the 10 Rings terrorists and his final battle against Iron Monger. **VERDICT: 20/20**

# **Characters**

# The characters are the worst aspect of the movie, yet they still work amazingly. Ho Yinsen is Iron Man’s mentor. He has clear character qualities, such as his compassion and loyalty to Stark. J.A.R.V.I.S. is Tony Stark’s AI system, and while he has no character traits or development, that is a good thing here. This is because he is an AI in a movie about humans. What character arc would he have? Happy Hogan is Stark’s bodyguard, and his loyalty charms me a lot. He is very kind to him, and I love it, because it elevates the humanity of the film. Pepper Potts is Tony’s assistant, and she has a real charm I do not know how to describe. As well, she knows how to be important for the story. My only gripe is that the female characters get very underutilized. Obadiah “Iron Monger” Stane is the main antagonist. Now, I do wish that he was not as predictable as he was, but he serves as a great mirror to Stark and who the man could have become. But then you have the protagonist. Tony “Iron Man” Stark. He has an extremely good character arc of learning to put others before himself. Even at the start, he had good within, so you could still root for him. **VERDICT: 18/20**

# **Acting**

# The acting is some of the best in the MCU phase one. Shaun Toub is Ho Yinsen. Toug consistently takes his role very seriously, and perfectly captures the concerns and kindness. Paul Bettany is J.A.R.V.I.S. Bettany sounds so artificial, but here, he makes it work, because he plays someone who is artificial. Jon Favreau (The director of the movie) is Happy Hogan. Hogan moves near Downey Jr., which is insanely accurate to how a bodyguard would move like. Gwyneth Paltrow is Pepper Potts. Paltrow perfectly blends feminine beauty with the power to be her own character. Jeff Bridges in menacing as heck as Obadiah Stane and his acting with his tone and eyes is greatly done. However, Robert Downey Jr. as Tony Stark is the best role. His tone in speaking, his body movements, his eye movement, everything is done so well with this character. **VERDICT: 20/20**

# **Music**

# The music is amazing in Iron Man. First off, there is a heavy electric guitar and rock-and-roll approach. It sounds incredibly cool, and fits the tone and main character perfectly. This is because Iron Man is addicted to being a bad\*\*\*, the music sounds bad\*\*\*, so it is a 100% match. "Driving with the Top Down" is incredibly energetic, and fits the tone very well. "Gulmira jand "Arc Reactor" are incredibly action packed and narratively get the scenes so well. I refer to "Back in Black" as “A song with swag” and Iron Man is “A character with swag”, so a real match. I also love how Black Sabbath's "Iron Man" pays homage to the movie.

# **In Conclusion**, Iron Man (2008) is a classic of superhero movies that still holds up today. It blends its stakes, characters, visuals, acting, score and comedy quite well even with a couple of nitpicks, and it feels grand regardless of intention. I would recommend this movie to any superhero fan. **Final Score: 97%**
