Greetings. I am setting out to review all of the MCU movies before Avengers: Doomsday (2026). For the list, I started out with Iron Man (2008). The next film on the docket is **Avengers 2: Age Of Ultron (2015)**. One: this film was confused. Did it want to be a tight story, or set up future MCU movies? Two: the movie doubled its cast. It added Quicksilver, Scarlet Witch and Vision. As well, the film was shot in South Africa, South Korea, Italy, UK and America. Three: The original script was 195 minutes long for Hawkeye’s family and Hulk and Romanoff’s romance. But it got cut by 54 minutes. So here is what worked and what did not. **SPOILERS!
Story**
=======

# What I feel most disappointed about the movie is that the story is quite indecent. Now, it is not as unforgiving as Thor 2: The Dark World, but still not good enough, compared to the first movie. Now, there are some good aspects. One of them is the high stakes and some of the pacing. We spend enough time with the humans, so I actually care about the plot of Ultron trying to genocide humanity. That is very high stakes and it made me care. The comedy is humorous enough. Especially Agent Hill saying to Captain America, “He's fast and she's weird.” That is humorous and unpredictable because we had no idea she would say something like that. Lastly, it feels like an actual MCU movie, because it sets up Ragnorok, when Hiemdall narrates what will happen to Asgard in Thor 3: Ragnorok. Now, as to what makes it suck. There is one reason, but it is enormous. There are way too many stories in one, and none of them have enough time to establish themselves. You have the main plot of Ultron trying to murder every last human, Tony Stark’s Fear and PTSD, The Maximoff Twins’ Redemption Arc, Thor’s Quest, Hawkeye’s family, Bruce and Natasha’s Relationship, and a lot more. In other words, the story has too much filler, and none of the stories have enough time to work. Why would I give the story a good score, if none of the stories work? **VERDICT: 9/20**

# **Visuals**

# Finally, onto the aspects of the movie I do not dislike. The visuals is one of those aspects. In fact, I find it amazing. The only thing I do not like is the character design of Ultron. He looks so human, and that is the last thing I would describe him. He could have had a face like his drones, but no. It causes him to look kind of stupid. But if I disliked Ultron so much in terms of design, how do I love the visuals so much? Well, everything else works. For one, the CGI looks incredible. Examples are Wanda’s powers and the Chitauri in Tony’s mind. They look incredible, and I actually thought they were real! Then you have the lighting. Every scene is lit decently bright during the joyful scenes, especially the party. However, the dark scenes are mostly with Ultron with Klaue and the Maximoffs. The cinematography is also incredible. There is no shaky cam and no distracting CGI. That means every shot is easy to see, and my personal favorite is the heartfelt Natasha hugging Lila. But the real best aspect is the action. Each of them are appropriately violent and the choreography is awesome. My personal favorite is the Hulk vs the Hulkbuster. **VERDICT: 18/20**

# **Characters**

# The characters are a bit better than the plot, but they do miss the potential they had. Now, there are some good ones. Some are even great! But others? They kind of miss the mark by a noticeable amount. Let us start with the ones that actually work. Ultron is the main antagonist, and he has everything that could make him work in terms of writing. He has a clear motive: the genocide of humanity, his reason of trying to evolve the earth makes sense, and he works as an actual threat. I would actually refer to him as the most underrated MCU villain of all time. Vision is up next and he absolutely rocks! In fact, I would consider him as my favorite character of this movie. He has a strong and actually well-paced character arc of discovering who he is. His future girlfriend Wanda is up next. I am glad they did not cram her entire arc into one movie, but she does have a well-realized arc of letting go of her parents’ loss. Finally, Hawkeye is the emotional anchor of the movie. I mean, he convinces someone as broken as Wanda to let go so easily, with a great speech. This makes me actually start to care about the story. But everyone else? Not so much. Iron Man still has his PTSD, after almost dying. Huh? I thought it was resolved in Iron Man 3 (2013)! Captain America is up next. I mean, what is there to say about him? He just wants to do the right thing all the time. The same can be said with Thor, but at least he discovers Ragnorok, so that is something. Bruce and Black Widow’s romance had an interesting idea. Hulk is obsessed with Black Widow, and Natasha is the one who keeps Hulk calm. But the execution is just underdeveloped mess. Quicksilver is last, and there is nothing to him. He starts off as a villain, turns good and then dies from a bullet he should be faster than! Gosh! **VERDICT: 12/20**

# **Acting**

# If I am being honest, the acting is the only aspect of the entire movie I absolutely would give that 10/10 to. Robert Downey Jr. Is Iron Man. Robert Downey Jr. as Tony Stark is the best role. His tone in speaking, his body movements, his eye movement, everything is done so well with this character. However, he also sells the nervousness of Stark without seeming childish. Even if it can get repetitive in terms of writing, the acting sells the showing, not telling even better. Chris Evans is Steve “Captain America” Rogers. Evans does a perfect job at both feeling like a leader and like an actual person. Especially here. Mark Ruffalo is Bruce “Hulk” Banner. Ruffalo captures the intelligence of The Hulk so well, but at the same time, he has you covered in the fear department. Scarlett Johansson is Natasha “Black Widow” Romanoff. Johansson perfectly enhances the concern, dedication, and compassion of her character very well. That might be hard to do, but she does it in spades. Jeremy Renner is Clint “Hawkeye” Barton. Renner has more lines, and says them perfectly. Like an actual mentor. Elizabeth Olsen and Aaron Taylor-Johnson are Wanda and Pietro Maximoff. The two of them have amazing chemistry with each other, but can actually feel like their own characters. Paul Bettany is Vision. Bettany gives such a philosophical performance, and has the perfect voice for his character. Finally, James Spader is Ultron. Spader gives the best portrayal of all, because of how complex it is. He perfectly gets the mixture between robotic and cunning. **VERDICT: 20/20**

# **Music**

# After two Marvel Cinematic Universe movies with perfect music, this one can come off as disappointing. For one, the sound effects are just… bad. We get a lot of drones whirring, and A: it happens too much, and B: it can be a bit too unsettling. Also, the music can be too loud. I sometimes barely hear the dialogue. Now, onto why it is really good, no matter what. One: Ultron’s theme goes hard. Yes, it is too loud, but I can still hear the robotic intensity. Two: it always works in the film’s narrative favor. Every beat works to enhance the stakes or make the softer scenes hit hard. If you consistently do that, Brian Tyler and Danny Elfman, you result in doing a great job. Three: each protagonist has a theme. Tony “Iron Man” Stark: Rock. Steve “Captain America” Rogers: Patriotism. Hulk: Horror. Thor: Energy. **VERDICT: 16/20**

# **To conclude**, Avengers 2: Age Of Ultron is conflicting. On one hand, the writing ranges from indecent to adequate. But everything else slaps! So the result makes this movie pretty good. You should watch it if you like the first. **Final Score: 75%**
