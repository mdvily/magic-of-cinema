# Greetings. I am setting out to review all of the MCU movies before Avengers: Doomsday (2026). For the list, I started out with Iron Man (2008). The next film on the docket is **Doctor Strange (2016)**. Filmmaker Scott Derrickson spent his own money to create a 12-page scene, concept art, storyboards, and an animatic based on the comic Doctor Strange: The Oath. He presented this in a 90-minute pitch to Marvel Studios just to prove his dedication. Derrickson also wanted to show the script but Jon Spaihts was given the position instead, Mr. Derrickson locked as director. That, and Cumberbatch almost did not make the role due to scheduling conflicts. So the film was delayed by four months to give Cumberbatch a more clear structure.

# **Story**

# The storytelling is great but it could be better. One: the story can be formulaic. It is just about an arrogant person who endures trauma, becomes something more and makes mistakes. We see this for almost every underdog story. And this one does nothing different. This can result in it being very dull and generic. But that is about it. Everything else is great. The comedy is humorous and the heart means something. Now, normally, this could potentially make the film tonally inconsistent. However, the film has the perfect balance and puts both in for the right scenes. The pacing is surprisingly great. Every scene takes as long as it should and then casually moves on to the next scene. Lastly, the climax is great. There is no final battle, just Doctor Strange outsmarting Dormammu, in a clever way. **VERDICT: 16/20**

# **Visuals**

# The visuals of Doctor Strange (2016) are perfect, on so many levels. For one, the time travel and universes have a lot of attention to detail. Examples are with the black shades of Dormammu and the dark dimension, and multiple reflections of the characters in the mirror dimension. Speaking of which, the color palette is some of the most beautiful of any movie I have ever seen. Not only does the film use every single color of the rainbow, but it does so effectively. The VFX is used very well, especially with magic, such as the Eldritch Magic. It looks incredibly detailed, and the main color of orange surprisingly looks really good. The cinematography is easy to look at, and has some excellent shots, such as every one in the final battle. Lastly, the action is well-choreographed, and the magic helps to bring it the extra mile. I think an example for me is the aerial fight with Kaecilius, because you have no idea whether Strange will live or die. **VERDICT: 20/20**

# **Characters**

# Many people think the characters are great and I respectfully disagree. I find them just bad. Doctor Stephen Strange is our main protagonist. And he absolutely sucks. It is like the MCU has no idea whether to make him arrogant or wisdomous. These ideas clash all the time, and it gives me whiplash. The Ancient One is the character with the most potential. She could have been iconic and crucial to the story, but she has no character traits and dies before she can leave an impact on anyone. Wong is Stephen’s main sidekick. I feel like he has the best character arc. He learns to accept Strange, and be less hard on him. Dr. Palmer is Strange’s colleague. And I absolutely love her compassion towards him. It results in the movie getting an emotional anchor, and a strong one. Now we are back to the characters who suck. Mordo is up next, and I dislike the direction they are leading him into betraying Strange. Yes, this happens in the end, but then what happens to him? Lastly, Kaecilius is the antagonist. He is one of the worst of the entire MCU. He just wants to destroy this universe because he made a deal with the devil. He does nothing special with it, whatsoever. At least the side characters make the world feel lived in. **VERDICT: 10/20**

# **Acting**

# The actors really work hard to get into who their characters are with their gestures and voices. And for the most part, they succeed. Benedict Cumberbatch is Doctor Stephen Strange. I like this actor in quite some roles, but this is not one of them. Cumberbatch really gives off a disappointing performance because he gets the body movements of a confused man very well but his voice? He never does anything special with it. He just sounds like Mr. “Can I Go Home Now?” Tilda Swinton is The Ancient One. And this is the beginning of me listing actors of the movie I think did an excellent job. Swinton perfectly captures the gravitas of a wise and regal mentor. But at the same time, she can change the tone in her voice, especially as her character gets upset with Strange. Benedict Wong is Wong (That is actually his character’s name.) Wong can have fierce body gestures at Cumberbatch, but can also have softer tones at him. This symbolizes how Wong can have a lot of range in this role. Rachel McAdams is Dr. Christine Palmer. McAdams perfectly nails the softness and care she has for Cumberbatch, even if she could deliver more emotion. Chiwitel Ejiofor is Mordo. For this role, I want you to imagine a parallel to Benedict Wong’s, only more dark with no soft moments. That matches his character perfectly. Ejofor is not the greatest actor ever, but he can deliver some creepy performances. Mads Mikkelsen is Kaecilius. If you thought that Cumberbatch had little personality, at least he had any. Mikkelsen has nothing. And his body movements are also matching to a spirited conqueror. That is not even remotely his character. **VERDICT: 16/20**

# **Music**

# When you think of the Marvel Cinematic Universe at its best music, what comes to mind? For me, it is this one. The score was composed by the legendary Michael Giacchino, who you might remember from PIXAR, especially The Incredibles (2004) and Up (2009). He does a mind-bending job here. I think we must start out with the instruments Giacchino used, such as a harpsichord, celeste, sitar and the tabla. This gives the film a trippy vibe that matches the visuals and effects of Doctor Strange. You would also have to factor in the main themes. You have Doctor Strange’s main tracks. His main theme is bold and soaring, that represents his growth from an arrogant surgeon, to a heroic magician. The cues fit his character traits, especially sarcasm and dedication. The Ancient One’s main theme is majestic and authentic, which compliment her wisdomous personality. Third, you have the slower, more emotional tracks, such as "The Hands Dealt". I praise this piece of the album because it slows down the story and is soft, just like the scene. This means Giacchino can expand his music volume. For the villain theme, you have a large, chanting choir that shows us the scope of the movie and the gothic chanting creates a mysterious cue for these characters. Lastly, superhero movies can feel like just “Hero Noises”. That is not the case for Doctor Strange. It has its own style of a mystic orchestra that meshes with the film very well. **VERDICT: 20/20**

# **In conclusion**, Doctor Strange (2016) is a great film. It is very close to a seal of approval, and almost a masterpiece. It just falters a bit, but definitely does more good than bad. **Final Score: 84%**
