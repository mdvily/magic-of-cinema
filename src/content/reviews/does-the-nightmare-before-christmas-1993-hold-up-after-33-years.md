# **The Nightmare Before Christmas (1993)**. What ended as an acclaimed Burton/Selick feature started as a poem. While the poem is too long to say, it is about the pumpkin king Jack Skellington, who gets tired of Halloween and tries to take over Christmas. I must say, I quite enjoyed the poem. At the start, Disney actually rejected it, due to this idea being too dark for younger audiences. On the other hand, they did come around after digging deep into Burton’s filmography. However, they only came around after the successes of Beetlejuice (1988) and Batman (1989). Although, since Burton was busy directing Batman Returns (1992), Henry Selick took the wheel. Finally, the songs were written by Danny Elfman. The film premiered on October 13, 1993, and officially hit theaters 26 days later. Fans enjoyed it back then, but now, they see it as an absolute phenomenon. But is this film a creative miracle, or just hauntingly bad? **SPOILERS!**

# **Story**

# Jack Skellington is having a midlife crisis, due to him expecting more to do every year. So he kidnaps Santa Claws and realizes his ungratefulness. Now, there are two flaws. One: watching the movie, I knew everything that was going to happen. The film thinks its charm means it can be as predictable as can be, and that is not true. Two: the romance between Jack and Sally happens at the very end. This means we have no time for development, so it gets predictable. Now, on to the positives! One: for centuries, Jack has had to be a part of the same of Halloween. I find it very relatable for him to want something new. The film paints Halloween and Christmas in a very respectful manner, and the blending is seamless. This leads to a perfect ending, where it finally snows in Halloweentown. The comedy is humorous and great for the source material and the characters. For example, Lock, Shock and Barrel kidnap the Easter Bunny, thinking it is Santa. It serves as a break for the horror, is in line with the characters, and is just funny. The heart, though, is the second most powerful aspect here. Jack’s loyalty to Zero makes me cry every time. Then, you have the theme of gratitude, which is amazing for a movie like this. **VERDICT: 16/20**

# **Animation**

# The movie looks perfect to me. First off is the color palette. For most of Halloween town, you have orange and blue, which prove the warmth, yet horror. Orange and Blue are complementary on the color wheel, so they mesh well here. Then, to look sinister, you have green as the main color. Its hue is to look sinister, and it works. Then you have the textures. Every location, such as Halloweentown, Boogie’s lair, or through the Christmas door has excellent detail, and it looks like the animators were very passionate, which they were. Yes, the film looks choppy, but that is a good thing here, because it meshes with the tone very well. But the character design and animation is my favorite aspect here. Every character is recognizable, with a keen attention to detail. The animation is shot at 24 FPS, meaning for every second, there are 24 frames on average. That means the animators worked themselves very hard. **VERDICT: 20/20**

# **Characters**

# The characters are vibrant, original and full of life and heart, even if they could be thought out better. Jack Skellington is our main protagonist. He wants to do something else in his life, and finds Christmas as a brilliant idea. He is very charming, and the idea to make him a misguided protagonist and not evil at heart was a nice choice. I also think he has a relatable midlife crisis. My problem? He learns his mistake in just one scene, which feels unearned. Maybe, he could make a mistake at the start, everything goes south and he has a slow burn character arc? That never happens. The character he kidnaps is Santa Claws, or as he calls him, “Sandy Claws”. Santa is charming here, and has that “Ho ho-ho!” Christmas spirit, but is barely utilized in the movie. Sally is someone who cares for him, and is very loving to him, but worried. That makes her three dimensional, but in a different way. Oggie “The Boogieman” Boogie is the villain, and I kind of like him , but to a 6.5/10 at best and 6 at worst. Yes, he is very humorous and campy, but he hardly does anything to move the story forward or even be important. Zero is adorable and my favorite character of the movie, because he is very significant to the story and serves as a great catalyst to his master’s good qualities. Finally, you have The Mayor, Jack’s funny and worried friend, and Lock, Shock and Barrel, his henchmen. They help the world feel lived in, and have plenty of character themselves. **VERDICT: 15/20**

# **Acting**

# The acting is great, full of cast members who get their roles, but sometimes they overact or underact. Chris Sarandon is Jack Skellington. Sarandon has a lot of charisma and curious energy as Jack. You really buy him as a kindhearted, yet misguided figure, but his overacting can make some lines feel a bit over the top. The late Edward Ivory portrayed Santa Claus. Some of his lines are underacting, but for the most part, he really gets Santa’s annoyance turned into fear. Catherine O'Hara, who sadly hit the great beyond last January 30, portrayed Sally. You get the gentleness and desire for a better life O’Hara gives her character and does not overact, but for an abused woman, her voice is a bit too soft. Still a good performance. Ken Page is Oggie “The Boogieman” Boogie. Page can have a delightfully sinister voice, but overacts a lot. Seriously, he sounds unstable at times. Frank Welker is Zero, my favorite portrayal of the movie. Walker makes his character sound so cute and loving, without the necessity to utter a single word. Glenn Shadix is the mayor. Shadix sounds so happy, yet can be serious when need be. Finally, you have Paul Reubens as Lock, Catherine O’Hara as Shock, and Danny Elfman as Barrel. The three both love Jack and are scared of him. And these three actors perfectly showcase this. **VERDICT: 16/20**

# **Music**

The music is wonderful here. Every single beat matches the scene and its severity pretty well. It feels like it deserves and has to be in these scenes. It can support epic and tender scenes and make it work. And the main theme to me is just iconic. But let us be honest: even with a great score, the music would be just good and not amazing without the songs. "Jack's Lament" is Jack expressing his boredom for life, and the desire for something new. Elfman does a great job at conveying this, and it is very important for this story. "Town Meeting Song" and "Jack's Obsession" showcase how the town of Halloween thinks Jack has gone mad, and Jack’s worries if they would help him with this idea and how this holiday even works. This means that they were diligent enough to show two sides, instead of one. “Kidnap the Sandy Claws" is Lock, Shock, and Barrel scheming on how to kidnap “Sandy Claws”. The song is fun, but did they need to turn it into a musical number? Not really. "Making Christmas", however, is the song that gets this movie’s premise the best. Now, while I do not hate "Oogie Boogie's Song", as it does sound catchy, there is no reason for it to be there. "Sally's Song" is beautiful, and perfectly showcases her love and fear for him. This makes her very three dimensional. "Poor Jack" is Jack realizing his flaw, and working to fix the mess he has made. And then there is "Finale / Reprise". This to me is as beautiful an ending you can get for this film. But my two favorite songs in this movie are "This Is Halloween" and "What's This?". They understand the intention of fun so well, and they serve as pivotal moments for the feature. **VERDICT: 18/20
In conclusion**, The Nightmare Before Christmas (1993) is a classic and a delightful holiday movie. Now, do I find this as a complete masterpiece? No, I find it somewhat overrated, but that is just my opinion, and I still love this movie. **Final Score: 85%**
