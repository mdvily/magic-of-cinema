**Cars (2006)**. Who ever would guess that Pixar’s next franchise after Toy Story, Monsters, Inc., Finding Nemo and The Incredibles, Cars would be their next franchise. Seriously, this movie is addicted to that idea, that the theme is about cars, every character is a car and even the title is only, “Cars”. And at the same time, in 2006, the film made many children grow up with Lightning McQueen. Cars out of all the Pixar golden age movies, considered to be the weakest. And while I certainly agree, I find it a great movie nonetheless! Yes, the sequels do not deserve to even be here, but the original does. Cars is about a racer for the piston cup, Lightning McQueen, as he stray away from the track, and causes a mess at radiator springs. As punishment, he is assigned community service, and learns to focus less on his achievements in life, and more on just living it. Now, it gets a review for its 20th year anniversary, I want to give it a mention.

**Story**

The movie was built to be about racing, and yet that was only a draw to bring in the audience. While there is a reason for the race and McQueen is a racer, the plot would rather give higher attention to the message. As a weakness, the storytelling can come off as relatively weak. The story follows the premise “Jerk with everything has nothing but the chance to be kind and make new friends. However, the key aspect is execution. And for the execution other than the characters being cars… there is nothing. The film’s comedy also does not help. The feature’s humor ranges from cute to absolutely absurd. Also, McQueen was so arrogant in the race with no redeeming qualities that he kind of deserved everything that happened to him. And it never really gets balanced out by the other characters being one-dimensional. However, the film wants less to do with having a solid story and more of being a tribute. Making the movie, Pixar wanted to pay homage to Route 66. Instead of taking the highway or intersection, you would find themselves in a quirky small town as a stop, where you would meet kind people with their own quirks. The film is trying to honor them. And that is my favorite aspect of this picture. It wants you to know about radiator springs, and it does a great job. It also gives us the “enjoy the small things”. This makes the film very heartfelt. **VERDICT: 13/20**

**Animation**

Pixar was given the mission to make a movie all about cars, and their first instinct was to do so literally I. This movie is a film where every character, animal, bug and even environment aspects are shaped as vehicles, such as trucks and helicopters. This made the movie’s budget of $120 million big enough to look grand but small enough to not have an overdone aesthetic. You can really tell how hard the film tried for you to look out for what would turn into an automobile, or what aspect would turn into a car. I would say that the film does that very well, and never feels like it goes off the rails. Yes, the film never explores a whole lot of its world, but this was the debut and it had to start somewhere. I must as well factor in how the non-car related aspects, such as the locations, and the 1960’s feel were as immersive as they were well-crafted, and they were excellent at both. Then, there is the cars and how they look. I will say this: It is very easy for you to tell which character is which character and the cars themselves fit flawlessly between cartoon expressions and rendering to accurate cars. And as great as the character designs are, I feel like we have had too much of a good thing. I question the characters’ anatomy. [#1.](#1.) When they stick out their tongues out. I start to wonder if they  have internal organs that are organic and not mechanic. Also, some of the racing scenes look too dark for a lighthearted movie. What I mean is that the characters get gruesomely injured, in ways that are not appropriate for children to see, and this film was made for children. But my favorite aspect is the color, and how it makes the characters look questionable. **VERDICT: 18/20**

**Characters**

A part of a Route 66 experience is to meet the locals who live them. The locals in the movie correspond to the characters. While some of them are charming, however, plenty of them are one-dimensional. Now, the characters are not bad, but nor are they that good. At least they are significant to the racing world. Rather they be the racers themselves, the racing announcers or the audience. The movie is like a racing Easter egg hunt. But onto the characters themselves, Lightning McQueen is the protagonist, and a real jerk who thinks that he is going to be the champion. If it were not for numerous situations that ruin his chances. But before he accepts his friends, man, does Pixar make him unlikable. He is the embodiment of selfishness. In fact, he is mean to everyone, referring himself as the most important car there is. That attitude is what ruins the mood of the picture. They could have made him comical, but nobody thought of that. Yes, he becomes kinder, but then it is too late and very hard to just wash off the spite I had for him. The roles of the side characters is not for them to drive the movie forward and for them to bring more life. You have Luigi, the Italian race car fanatics, Ramon, the body paint artist who always brags about his work, Flo, the diner owner, Sarge, the army man who always argues argues with Fillmore, the town’s hippie, Red the firetruck, Sheriff, the town’s policeman and Lizzie, the oldest resident of Radiator Springs. However, there are three other characters that I actually liked. First one is Mater, who sucked in the sequels, but was humorous and charming in this movie. He is dumb in a way where he is naive and just enjoys life, a great catalyst to McQueen’s character arc. You have Sally, McQueen’s love interest, who has a character arc of warming up to him. And then there is Doc Hudson, who resents McQueen. But the reason why is very fascinating. You see, Hudson was a racer himself and he treats Lightning like what he felt like as a racer. What I love about himself the most is his character journey of learning to accept his past. Also, Chick Hicks was a funny yet generic villain. **VERDICT: 10/20**

**Acting**

Owen Wilson is Lightning McQueen. McQueen is an unlikable jerk, and Wilson takes advantage of that. He sounds like a real hothead, and actually becomes more charming. Larry the Cable Guy is Mater. Larry is humorous, and cares about how stupid his character is. He really gets at the same time how kind and friendly his character is. Bonnie Hunt is Sally Carrera. Hunt is casual-talking in this role, and she gets a voice softer and softer as the film goes on. The late Paul Newman gives my favorite performance in the movie as Doc Hudson. He sounds so authentic, but like an actual character. The side cast such as Joe Ranft, Tony Shalhoub, Katherine Helmond, Cheech Martin, George Carlin and others makes the world feel lived in and fully understands their characters. Finally, you have Michael Keaton as Chick Hicks. Keaton has so much energy, and is so easy to hate in a good way. **VERDICT: 20/20**

**Music**

The music is wondrous. It perfectly captures what this movie is about in every single scene. "Sh-Boom" is used by the twilight drive scene. It sounds beautiful and has a nostalgic vibe. "Our Town" is the emotional song, that makes you know what a ballad of softness it is. "Real Gone" is the opening track that wonderfully gets you to know what the movie is like. But then you have "Life Is a Highway". "Life Is a Highway" has such accurate lyrics to the movie, such as Life is a highway “I wanna ride it all night long/ If you're goin' my way/ Well, I wanna drive it all night long“ and the volume is perfect. And then you have the score. The score is nothing less of amazing. The volume is perfect. The film is never too loud or too quiet. The balance between the sound design and score is great, and neither of them get overshadowed. Lastly, it mixes traditional Americana and orchestral themes with touches of blues and electric guitar to match the setting of small-town and stock-car racing. That is a very bold choice that panned out well. **VERDICT: 20/20**

**In conclusion**, Cars (2006) is a great movie that deserves more love. Yes, the writing could be very much improved, but the animation, acting and music is all amazing. If you like vehicles, I would absolutely recommend this to you. **Final Score: 81%**
