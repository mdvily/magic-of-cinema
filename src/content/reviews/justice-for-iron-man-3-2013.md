# Greetings. I am setting out to review all of the MCU movies before Avengers: Doomsday (2026). For the list, I started out with Iron Man (2008). The next film on the docket is **Iron Man 3 (2013)**. This is a movie that Marvel Cinematic Universe fans have been hating on for a long time. And just now, it seemed to receive a re-evaluation. So, naturally, here is why it deserves it. **SPOILERS!**

# **Story**

# The story is not as fantastic as the first, but considering how terrible the second one was written, this was a very big improvement. In other words, a good story we have here, indeed. What I love most is that the stakes are back. Iron Man’s fear of death works for three reasons: it makes sense after what happened after the climax of the last MCU movie. It gives him a clear character arc of bravery and courage. It elevates the stakes. Speaking of which, the stakes are off the roof. The film is a life or death scenario a lot of the time, and it made me care about what happened in Iron Man’s third solo chapter. However, there is comedy not to kill the film’s mood, but to let you know that this is an Iron Man movie. A central part to Mr. Stark is that there should be a decent amount of humor. The jokes also got a laugh out of me, because they fit the characters. Lastly, the film is very well-paced. Every scene takes the perfect amount of time it should, and then move on to the next scene. However, I have two problems. One: the plot is fairly predictable. I thought something horrifying for the man in metal would happen, he would be a jerk for most of the movie and then be a hero in the end. That is exactly what happened. Second, does the film want to be about Killian’s crimes and how he must be stopped, or Tony realizing to face his fears. Both of the stories were written well, but I got whiplash because the film has no idea what to focus on. **VERDICT: 14/20**

# **Visuals**

# Would I say that this movie has the greatest filmmaking/animation of all time? No. Would I say it is a clear improvement over its predecessor? Absolutely. This movie looks awesome, with very little to complain about. First off, the weight of the objects, such as the Malibu mansion during its destruction looks and feels incredibly heavy. And with an environment around the Malibu mansion, it blends seamlessly. Second of all, the Mark 42 armor had different pieces that would snap, lock, and wrap around Stark’s body, and actually hurts the character. That makes sense, due to the speed of the objects hitting Downey Jr.’s body. Third: RDJ’s ankle actually sustained an injury during filming, so the VFX team was hired to make a body double for the actor. And it looks exactly like him. I honestly never knew it because it looked so precisely accurate to Robert’s body. And most of all, the color palette matches the tone perfectly. We got shades of bright red, yellow and gold for the first two movies. However, during the third, in order to match the mood, the color scheme went darker, into shades of black and dark blue. However, I do have two criticisms: One: the cinematography never supports the story. It just stays in one place, even when it could move around. That is lazy and boring. Also, the action scenes are poorly choreographed. I mean, it just looks like characters hitting each other and explosions. There is no rhythm. But get past that, this movie deserved to win the Oscar for best visual effects in 2014, and the fact that it did not? At least it was a nominee. **VERDICT: 16/20**

# **Characters**

# The characters are fantastic, and is the best of the trilogy. Tony “Iron Man” Stark is our main protagonist for the third time, and he just gets better and better. I love how his character arc consists of his near-death situation after Avengers (2012), where he focuses on Iron Man always having to be alive to protect other people. He gets so focused on Iron Man, that he ignores Tony Stark, especially in terms of him learning how to face his fears. Pepper Potts’ character arc is complete in this movie. She goes from just being an assistant to Stark to actually being his romantic partner. This consists of her learning to not just be dedicated to Tony, but to actually be there for him. Happy Hogan is Stark’s bodyguard, and his loyalty charms me a lot. He is very kind to him, and I love it, because it elevates the humanity of the film. But when he gets blown up, it serves as an important aspect of Stark’s growth. James “Rhodey” Rhodes is Iron Man’s main connection to the military and his kindness makes him charming and actually complex. He can either utilize his duty to the U.S. Military, or be there for his best friend. But if you think of a character from Iron Man 3, you would likely think of Harley. The boy is adorable and has humorous quirks with Mr. Stark. Also, his technology he uses to help the man with PTSD is creative. However, where the heck did he get the experience to use it? Aldrich Killian is the main antagonist, and you the trilogy really redeemed themselves after Whiplash. Killian has a personal motive: He wants to be better than Stark, even if it means getting him killed. Finally, Trevor Slattery is the fake villain. Let me just say, I cannot stand this character in the slightest. Not because he is not comic book accurate, an invalid criticism. But because he is offensive to actors. **VERDICT: 18/20**

# **Acting**

# If I have to say how Iron Man’s third outing is an improvement over his second, it would be the acting. In my opinion, Robert Downey Jr. as Tony Stark is the best role. His tone in speaking, his body movements, his eye movement, everything is done so well with this character. However, he also sells the nervousness of Stark without seeming childish. Gwyneth Paltrow is Pepper Potts. Paltrow has amazing chemistry with RDJ, and has a lot of boldness for her character alone. However, what makes her complex is that she is rather gentle in her body movements and voice occasionally, making her love for Stark feel noticeable. Jon Favreau portrays Happy Hogan. Favreau is so charming in this role, and his clumsiness always gets a chuckle out of me. Don Cheadle is James “Rhodey” Rhodes. Cheadle has a lot of great chemistry with Downey Jr. and he is humorous, yet respectable in this role. Ty Simpkins is Harley. Simpkims knows all the time how to sound charming. At the same time, he has bold movements and just an adorable face. Guy Pearce is Aldrich Killian. Pearce is insanely terrifying, but he does not do it with loud volumes. He does it with silence, and that is true fear. **VERDICT: 20/20**

# **Music**

# What I love a lot about Iron Man 3 is the music, and how it captures the intensity of every scene, but also the tender scenes. First off, the melody in the tender scenes, such as Tony with Harley is lifting. I cry during the scenes not only because of the character interractions, but because the music narrates the mood all too well. Speaking of which, the film knows quite well how to narrate what goes on. The film is loud during the action scenes and quiet during the softer moments, which makes sense. Another thing is that the film is never too loud or too quiet. You can hear every beat, but it never made my ears bleed, because score composer Brian Tyler knows how to contain himself. Tyler also meshes well together rock elements, piano, anvil strikes, and synthetic pulses, in order to give us a “free-falling” musical identity. This causes him to understand very well what happens in the story. Lastly, the songs have a link connection with the movie. Such as "Can You Dig It?"/fun, "Blue (Da Ba Dee)"/ 90’s in the prologue, and "Mambo No. 5" and "Jingle Bells"/holiday. These songs integrate both with Shane Black’s direction and Iron Man’s journey. My only gripe is that the action cues can get repetitive at times. **VERDICT: 19/20**

# **In conclusion**, Iron Man 3 is an underrated gem and a sleeper hit of an MCU movie. I honestly think that this is one of the best threequels of the MCU. So I would recommend this to anyone who likes the first two Iron Man movies. **Final Score: 87%**
