# Greetings. I am setting out to review all of the MCU movies before Avengers: Doomsday (2026). For the list, I started out with Iron Man (2008). The next film on the docket is **Captain America 2: The Winter Soldier (2014)**. The first thing that the developers did was that they paired Steve Rogers with Natasha Romanoff. They did not give him his supporting cast, because most of them died after the events of the first movie. Now, for a partner for Captain America, they almost put Clint “Hawkeye” Barton in the picture, but since he did not have enough to do, he was cut from the story. They could have brought back Red Skull. However, they wanted a grounded action movie villain, not a fantastical antagonist. The Russo brothers would then meet up with Ed Brubaker, who created the Winter Soldier storyline. Lastly, not only did Sebastian Stan return as The Winter Soldier, but Anthony Mackie also joined as The Falcon. **SPOILERS!**

# **Story**

# This story is something that fans and critics alike absolutely love, and I agree strongly. For one, the story is extremely unpredictable. I went into this film thinking I knew everything that would happen and I was so wrong about everything. I had no idea that HYDRA survived World War II, and is slowly destroying SHIELD. That is something I did not see coming. You next have the pacing. The film is not just rushed to the next action scene. Instead, the film perfectly takes recognition that is must take its time to establish the plot and the characters. It is a different choice from the first movie and a clear improvement. Every scene takes the time it needs and smoothly moves on to the next scene. The comedy is minimal, yet highly effective. The jokes are Steve’s out of time nature, and it perfectly showcases what it is like to be Steve Rogers, all while just being hilarious. The heart of the story is also amazing. I cried a dozen times. Especially when Steve tells Bucky that he is with him to the end of the line. That is absolute cinema. But the best part is that the film focuses on government surveillance, pre-crime algorithms, drone warfare, and the dangerous trade-off of civil liberties for security. Those are deep morals, executed to perfection. In conclusion, screenwriters Christopher Markus and Stephen McFeely did an excellent job at writing a powerful story, but did they do the same thing with the characters? **VERDICT: 20/20**

# **Visuals**

# I honestly think that the visuals are amazing, and really get slept on. First off, there is little CGI. However, there is a noticeable amount of VFX, such as The Falcon’s wings and the helicarriers. They look seamless with a noticeable attention to detail. Especially with three red lines on his suit’s shoulders. Then you have the style and color palette. The film takes on themes or gray and dark blue. Now, you might be thinking, “MovieMan, how do you like this color palette?” Well, I did not always. I originally thought it looked rather ugly, but on rewatch, I actually realizes that it complements the bleak tone very well, and fits the tension of the scenes. But during the ending, the color takes more colored shades which show that Captain America is at peace with himself. Third: The cinematography is awesome. The camera moves at an appropriate speed, and every shot has an appropriate amount of detail. Not a crazy amount, but a noticeable amount. And finally, this has some of the most intense action scenes of the MCU’s second phase. The choreography is excellent, the stunts are intense and while it is not too violent, there is a noticeable amount of bloodshed. These include The infiltration onto the cargo ship, Nick Fury’s car chase, Captain America’s escape from SHIELD, and the final battle, which just get better and better. **VERDICT: 20/20**

# **Characters**

# The characters are easily the most praised aspect of the movie, and my favorite aspect. Steve “Captain America” Rogers is our protagonist, and he is still incredible. Rogers has a strong character arc of adapting to and finding his place in the new world. He is charming, and still has the leadership qualities you need to be the best of the best: Integrity, Nobility and Compassion. However, he still has his stubbornness. Natasha “Black Widow” Romanoff is the secondary protagonist, and she also has a strong movie character arc that helps as a part of her real character arc. She realizes that in order to escape from her mistakes, she cannot just work in SHIELD, but has to do good things outside of the agency. Such as her friendship with Captain America. James Buchanan “Bucky” Barnes/The Winter Soldier is the “villain” of the movie. I say “villain” loosely, because technically, he is not one. He is brainwashed, so it is not his fault he is doing these things. However, he also has a strong character arc on realizing who he is. A key example is when he saves Captain America’s life near the end. Sam “The Falcon” Wilson is Captain America’s friend, and is incredibly likable. He joins the Marvel Cinematic Universe with flying colors, due to his compassion, tough personality, and his refusal to give up. Nick Fury is upsetting here. Not because he is a bad character, in fact, this is the best he has ever been. But because he sucks after this movie. What I love most about him is how he has a character arc of changing his ideals in order to keep the world safe. Alexander Pierce is the actual villain, and he is basically an opposite to Nick Fury. He as well feels like an actual threat, and compromising S.H.I.E.L.D. is a very complex and high-stakes story. Lastly, Brock Rumlow is the secondary villain, and him being a twist antagonist is something I never saw coming. **VERDICT: 20/20**

# **Acting**

# Maybe the acting is not as neglected as the visuals, but I still feel like it does not get the love it deserves. Chris Evans is Steve “Captain America” Rogers. Evans does a perfect job at both feeling like a leader and like an actual person, even in the sequel. He nails the empathy of his character, and has excellent chemistry with Johansson. Scarlett Johansson is Natasha “Black Widow” Romanoff. Johansson is so charming, yet says her lines with real sincerity. She knows who her character is, and the body movements are casual turned into action-packed. Just like her character. Sebastian Stan is James Buchanan “Bucky” Barnes/The Winter Soldier. Stan is the definition of a badass in this role. During the fight scenes, he says very little, only once asking who Bucky is. But behind that tough facade, there is an actual amount of emotion in his role. Anthony Mackie is Sam “The Falcon” Wilson. Mackie perfectly sells two things. Those two thing are his chemistry with Evans, and his undeniable compassion. Samuel L. Jackson is Nick Fury. Jackson plays a very intense character with a quiet yet commanding presence. An example is when he shoots dead Alexander Pierce. Speaking of which, Pierce is portrayed by Robert Redford. Redford is one of the greatest actors of all time, but gets no leeway just for his iconic status. However, he does not need it. Redford is quiet, and calculating. This really helps to make him the equivalent to an Anti-Nick Fury. And when he says “Hail HYDRA” for his final words, it is terrifying. Frank Grillo is Brock “Crossbones” Rumlow. Grillo perfectly gets the stealth reveal of Rumlow and perfectly says his lines and acts with his eyes. **VERDICT: 20/20**

# **Music**

# Oh, my goodness, they absolutely cooked with the music. Every beat belongs in this movie, because it works in tandem with the writing. It would not make any sense for the pulses to be absent. First off: the music is quiet enough for you to hear the characters. Two examples are when Black Widow orders the civilians to leave the streets as she fights Bucky, or when Buck pounds Steve to the ground. You can hear every word, every beat, and yet every cue. The music is still loud enough to be heard. Such as S.H.I.E.L.D.’s attack on HYDRA’s cargo ship. Especially when Rogers leaps down from the quintjet without a parachute. Second of all: The music works in tandem with the story. Every single beat feels like it is there to tell us where the plot is going, such as mysterious, yet tense music as Natasha tells Steve who The Winter Soldier is. That perfectly showcases how tense the scene is, and since we have no idea who he is, so it keeps it mysterious. Third: Each character has a motif. Captain America’s is Pompous, loud enough and cheerfully optimistic, Black Widow’s is energetic, yet soft at times and Nick Fury’s is low-tuned and chaotic with a rhythm. And fourth: The movie composer, Henry Jackman successfully mixes the music with the sound effects, like Bucky catching the shield. **VERDICT: 20/20**

# **In conclusion**, Captain America: The Winter Soldier (2014) is an absolute Star-Spamgled masterpiece. It honestly surprises me how the MCU went from making my least favorite of their movies at the time, to my favorite of the time so fast, but you get what you get. I start to feel like watching this film every Memorial Day, and that is because it is even more patriotically powerful than the first movie, so I would recommend it to anyone over 13. **Final Score: 100%**
