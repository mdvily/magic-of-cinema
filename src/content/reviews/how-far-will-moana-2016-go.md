**Moana (2016)**. After the unexpected success that was Zootopia (2016), Disney had returned again that year, but there was a difference between the two films. When we went into Zootopia, did anyone have a clue that this movie would be so good? Not really? But with Moana, I think you know what to expect. After all, it looks like it could follow more than the traditional formula. What I mean is, Disney found a formula in Tangled (2010), and it worked so well, Disney has been doing it almost ever since. But when did directors John Musker and Ron Clements start working on this project? Well, originally, there would be a subplot of Moana with six brothers. They would be in a canoe race, and the victor would become the new chief of Motunui. And yet, to focus on their sister, the brothers were struck from the plot, never to be seen again. Put was as well meant to be Moana’s only companion. Fortunately, that did not happen. But you know what did happen? Maui was stripped the title of the protagonist, and that went to Moana, who was supposed to be a die-hard Maui fan. Lastly, the directors took five years learning about Polynesian culture, to make it accurate. This was their sixth film. The other five were The Great Mouse Detective (1986), The Little Mermaid (1989), Aladdin (1992), Hercules (1997), Treasure Planet (2002) and The Princess and the Frog (2009). However, this was their first CG animated feature. But was sailing to this film worth it? **SPOILERS!**

**Story**

Many people have criticized this movie for being too much like The Little Mermaid (1989), and I can see why. Moana is the daughter of the king, or chief, here, who wants more in her life, but to keep his daughter safe, her father refuses. However, there are clear differences. Ariel wants more from her life. Moana wants to save her people, because the ocean chose her. Triton actually tried ruining his daughter’s mission by destroying her hideout. Tui never did that. He just tried convincing her not to go, and when she made up her mind after his mother’s death, he did not stop her. But then, this proves to be one of Disney’s most original movies in recent years. We have never seen a Disney movie about the two leads sailing from point A to point B with execution like this. What I mean is, Disney uses its setting as a means for the character growth. Whether it be Maui learning to live up to the legend, or for Moana to come as the savior. The heart if very strong, with characters actually opening up to each other, and even being there for each other. The message of letting yourself listen to the inner voice and not letting fear stop you from doing what you find in your heart right is inspiring, and it makes for a very different character arc to our protagonist. But my favorite aspect is the setting. There is plenty of lore and backstory to this picture, such as the opening minutes explaining why someone must restore Te Fiti’s heart. We even learn how Moana’s ancestors did not always live in Motunui, and what they did before voyaging there, by showing, rather than telling. My only gripe is that the comedy is kind of a mixed bag. There are some funny jokes, such as Maui referencing Disney princesses, and unfunny jokes as Hei Hei eating the heart of Te Fiti. **VERDICT: 19/20**

**Animation**

The animation here is absolutely beautiful in every regard, to the point where this is my favorite animation of any of these movies. First off, the color palette. The sailing and calm scenes are blue, with a mix of green if it is a fight between the sailor and the demigod, purple during scary segments, like the realm of monsters, AKA Lalotai, and red during the action scenes, like Moana Vs. Te Ka. This perfectly helps you know which sort of scene you are in. Then, you have the character animation. Every character moves so fluidly, from the humans and animals to the monsters. Especially the ocean. Now, animating water is very hard to do, so the animators had to work hard to make it work. And they did so by building a custom program called Splash specifically to simulate how water particles interact with characters and their environment. It works very well, because it feels real, and that it had to be done in animation. We now arrive at the geology, and it looks really accurate. You have their grass and trees, their ocean and beaches, the sunlight of the place, everything. That means, not only was it bound to work, but that Disney put in effort, and a lot of it. As well, Mini Maui looks 2D animated, because he actually was. Eric Goldberg made him 2D, and it blends seamlessly on a 3D human. The action scenes and dances from Moana’s people are very well-choreographed. Every scene has a wonderful amount of detail in it, but my favorite aspect is the character designs. The designs look very complicated to make, and the accuracy is unreal. Te Ka looks like an actual threat, not just a very large lava monster. Tamatoa fits that blend of half crab, half monster. The Kakamora look cute at first, fierce later. The list goes on! **VERDICT: 20/20**

**Characters**

Okay, so the characters are my least favorite part of the movie. But how high of a bar is that? Very low, because everything else is masterful, and so is this aspect. Moana herself is our main protagonist. She has a clear conflict: does she want to obey her father and help her people, or listen to her calling to her journey. But when the adventure begins, she does not simply forget about her family, as we even see them die in her nightmare. Her character arc of fully becoming a hero is exceptionally paced, and makes sense. My only gripe? She does not struggle a lot. Yes, her and Maui getting into fights is a thing here, but that is more Maui’s stubbornness than Moana’s mistakes. Maui is her main companion, and while he is full of life, he has an actual character arc of being more selfless, and cooperating with Moana. He makes mistakes, atones for them, and uses them to make him better. Hei Hei is her companion, and he is rather humorous. A key glimpse of character growth from him is that he goes from swallowing the heart of Te Fiti, to keeping it safe in his mouth from Te Ka. The Ocean is her other companion, and it can convey such annoyance at Maui, and pride of Moana, without the need for any words. It’s character arc, though is becoming nicer to the demigod. Te Ka/ Te Fiti is the scary antagonist, who proves to be menacing and pivotal for Moana’s development. But I have two flaws with her. One, since she lost her heart, her ability to do good deeds, of course she would turn evil. That excuses what she did, and does not explain it. Also, she has no clear character traits, making her kind of one-dimensional. Chief Tui and Sina are Moana’s parents, and they help the world feel lived in. They also give Moana her character conflict, and then letting her do the right thing is true character arcs for side characters. Gramma Tala is Moana’s grandmother. Tala is the one who guides Moana to start her journey, and gives us my favorite scene in the film. For the side antagonists, we have Tamatoa and the Kakamora. They serve as great obstacles, and one being scary, the other, funny, makes you know which one it is. Also, Schaffrillas Productions the YouTuber’s favorite character ever, is Tamatoa, so there. Another one who loves him. **VERDICT: 18/20**

**Acting**

The acting is incredible. Every actor puts in an appropriate amount of effort into their roles, without ever overacting. Auliʻi Cravalho is Moana. Cravalho has a soft voice, and sounds caring, yet can be tough and stern when need be. She perfectly captures every beat you would want from a dedicated heroine. Alan Tudyk is Hei Hei. Tudyk is no stranger to Disney, but this is his first role, where he says nothing, only using clucks and squawks. He clucks when he feels calm, but when he senses danger, he squawks. That is a noticeable attention to detail. Temuera Morrison and Nicole Scherzinger are Chief Tui and Sina. Morrison and Scherzinger perfectly capture their pride of their daughter, as well as the concern of her safety. But what sets them apart is Morrison’s stubbornness and Scherzinger’s pride. Rachel House is Gramma Tala. House in 2016 was only 44, as she portrayed her. However, she makes the most out of it. How? Not only does she sound very wise, but in her voice, I heard a sense of compassion and great chemistry with Cravalho. Jemaine Clement is Tamatoa. Clement sounds so sinister, but you can sense a lot of annoyance in his voice. Every actor just mentioned is amazing, but none of the compare to Dwayne “The Rock” Johnson as Maui. The Rock has so much energy and passion to make Maui feel full of life, but he also sells the arrogance and softer moments. **VERDICT: 20/20**

**Music**

The main score is amazing. There is a lot of orchestral authenticity in every beat, which all belong in this movie. But at the same time, some sound effects within the tracks sound Polynesian. This means that Disney cared a lot about the culture. But it is the songs that make the music so good. You have the songs that have no English words, such as, “Tua Tagaloa”, or “An Innocent Warrior”, and “Logo Te Pate”. They perfectly explain the mood of the scene. “An Innocent Warrior” shows the Ocean’s thoughts on this toddler, and “Logo Te Pate” demonstrates how Moana and Maui are officially on their way to save the day. "How Far I'll Go" and "How Far I'll Go (Reprise)” are Moana reflecting on her journey, and the lyric of Moana asking what is wrong with herself perfectly reflect on how she is unsure on what to do. "We Know The Way" and "We Know The Way (Finale)” are the Motunui residents explaining in great and appropriate detail what their culture is like. “You’re Welcome” is Maui boasting about how awesome he is. It is accurate to Maui as a legend of the people, and essential for his character arc. The “villain song” is “Shiny” and gives you a great metaphor between Maui and Tamatoa. Tamatoa brags about bad things and stays a villain. Maui brags about good things and becomes a hero. But if you want emotional songs, look no further than "I Am Moana" and "Know Who You Are". **VERDICT: 20/20**

**In conclusion**, Moana (2016) is a magical experience unlike any other from Disney. Yes, it has some writing issues, but all five categories are amazing nonetheless. I guess you could say that it went very far. If you are a fan of Disney or Polynesian mythology, this is a must see. **Final Score: 97%**
