# **The Dog Stars (2026)**. This film began by Lily Brooks Dalton (the author of The Midnight Sky) suggesting to director Ridley Scott the 2012 novel by Peter Heller. Scott agreed and decided to bring the aerial identity from The Sound Barrier (1952), because there were plane sequences in the book. Paul Mescal was supposed to be the actor of the main character, Hig, but because there were scheduling problems with Sam Mendes’ multi-part Beatles anthology project, he bailed out and was replaced by Jacob Elordi. The film was shot for only 34 days straight, with director Scott using up to 10 cameras at once to capture different angles, a well-known play from him. The script was written by Mark L. Smith, who wrote The Revenant back in 2015. However, Scott added scenes, such as Hig, Cima and Pops realize they were lead into a trap. The film was set to release on March 27, 2026. But it was released 5 months and a day later, on August 28. And now that the movie is officially in theaters, does this dog deserve a treat, or should this plane crash into Marauder territory? Now, I will try to avoid spoilers.

# **Story**

# The story here is obviously not that great, but when you think about it, there are some good qualities. First off, there are changes to the book. Now, while it does make it less faithful, I still like it because they make the film less predictable. Now, I cannot say what they are, but I can say they make the film feel more cinematic. Then, the heart is very prominent. Ever since Hig and Jasper separated, there have been moments of the characters actually being kind to one another, giving us a scary, yet warm tone. I would say that in my humble opinion, this is one of Scott’s most heartfelt movies. I must also factor in how the film is tonally consistent, with hardly any filler. For the other side of the story coin, you have the stakes. They must exist, because this is a survival movie, and they indeed do. The stakes are used to make you care and start worrying for the characters, and I would say that it works very well. Lastly, the film shows and does not tell in plenty of scenes, which is one of cinema’s golden rules. It trusts us to comprehend what is going on, and the dialogue is great. However, I do not like the story all that much. This is because of its lack of purpose. It has not a clue if it wants to be an action-packed thriller, or a soft and tender story. This can result in the film to not have fluid pacing, as well as poorly putting in scenes together. Also, the romance can feel very rushed, and the story unfinished. Finally, it introduces to us stuff like an apocalypse and sadness over losing a loved one. We have seen this a lot, and the film puts in absolutely nothing to give it originality. Now, when the story was criticized, the critics and audiences alike made good points I agree with, but they really left out what the story did well, such as the tone, heart and unpredictability. **VERDICT: 12/20**

# **Visuals**

# From this point forward, all of the five elements of the story were executed well. The visuals though, are almost its best aspect. First off, the cinematography is done by Erik Messerschmidt. And Mr. Messerschmidt, you sir did an amazing job. First off, the camera is shot at the characters’ heads during dialogue sessions, but the more action-packed segments have aerial shots, which let us know how serious the scenes are. The CGI, such as a character once being on fire, or the reapers looks great. This is because they went all out and made the film look as unreal, yet believable as they wanted to. And as Tim from a YouTube channel of “Empathy Machines” said out of exact words, a good aspect to tell a story is with weather. The action-packed scenes mostly have cloudy moods, and we see the stars and sunsets for calm scenes. The characters wear dark green military clothes. This perfectly shows us that not only are most of them in the military, but it complements the darker tone. Finally, the film was shot in Italy. Now, since the setting takes place in Colorado, they could have filmed it there, but it does look good in la campagna italiana. My only real criticism is with the color pallet. Everything looks very gray here. Now, yes, It does support the post-apocalyptic feel, but we should have seen more color to show us signs of hope. Fortunately, everything else works and if you only see movies that look good, this is for you. **VERDICT: 18/20**

# **Characters**

# The movie’s characters are not as good the visuals, acting and music, but are better than the story aspect. We begin with our main protagonist, Hig. Hig is a man grieving his dog, and as a result, starts to feel alone in the world. But he slowly starts to open up and accept that he is not alone. That is a very mature character arc, and him accepting connection feels human. Jasper is his dog, and the catalyst for Hig’s character arc. This makes him very important, and Jasper is just a good boy. My only downside is that since something happens to him pretty early, it stops him from actually getting to do something useful to drive forward the story. Cima is a character who sees Hig as a friend, not an outsider. I dislike her as a character, because she starts off kind and flawless, and then ends kind and flawless. There is no character arc of hers, and she is very one-dimensional. Like, other than kindness, she does not have a character trait. Bangley is Hig’s partner in war, and starts off as very verbally abrasive to him. This is because he has been hardened by the end of the world. But across the film, he becomes more kind. Pops is Cima’s father, and starts off cynical of Hig. He sees him as an outsider, but across the film, he becomes more accepting on who he is. The villains are the reapers. While we get absolutely zero backstory of them, they are pretty menacing. **VERDICT: 14/20**

# **Acting**

# In all honesty, we are nearing the greatest aspect of the movie, and the acting is a very close king to getting that throne. Jacob Elordi is Hig. Elordi understands perfectly how upset his character is, but can feel charming in a couple of scenes. This means that he backs up his voice and body movements with the ability to feel real. Raksha is Jasper. First, I am very glad that the dog never had a human to do the voice, and two, you can see how Jasper stays very close to Elordi, a true showmanship in bonds. Margaret Qualley is Cima. Her deep kindness towards Hig is very charming, and I did not mind how she never really yells. It actually helps with the tone. Josh Brolin is Bangley. He starts off feeling very stubborn and rude, but becomes more soft-spoken with softer body movements. This is even more evidence on his character arc. Guy Pearce is Pops. Pearce sounds very stubborn and cynical, with sharp body movements. But as the film progresses, it drops from him, being replaced by a kind voice and smooth movements. All of the actors do a great job, and my only flaw is that the voices can sometimes sound dry. **VERDICT: 19/20**

# **Music**

# The feature’s music was composed by Harry Gregson-Williams, and he did a fantastic job. First off, the film can sound grandiose and perilous at times, but mostly only when powerful elements happen. For the most part, there are calm scenes, so calm music plays. Every music beat is worked up to either by other beats or the story. This makes it feel destined to happen in a good way. My three favorite tracks, though, are “Heading to Grand Junction”, “Hig's First Flight” and “Bangley's Last Stand”. They sound intense, without being generic. But my favorite piece of the music that perfectly hits the film’s “heartfelt zone” is “Like Real People Do” by Hozier. It sounds so heartfelt, and perfect for the movie. My younger brother loves it so much and I agree. My only gripe is that sometimes intense themes play during non-intense scenes. **VERDICT: 19/20**

# **In conclusion**, Yes, The Dog Stars (2026) does indeed deserve the treat. It is an experience that perfectly understands how to make you feel, and the aesthetic is absolutely masterful. Yes, the writing is… not great, but there is plenty of good to it. If you like the book by Mr. Heller, you should give this film a go. **Final Score: 82%**
