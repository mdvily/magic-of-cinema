Greetings. I am setting out to review all of the MCU movies before Avengers: Doomsday (2026). For the list, I started out with Iron Man (2008). The next film on the docket is **Spider-Man 2: Far From Home (2019)**. This is the final MCU movie of the Infinity Saga, and the 23rd MCU movie. But does it stick the landing? **SPOILER ALERT!**\
**Story**\
Many people hate the writing of this film. And I ask you all, why? Because my only flaw is that the story is not at all thought together. But it dumbs the movie down and fails to understand its purpose. This movie could have been the characters being brainwashed, because it does not need to be comic book accurate. Instead, the writers did a lot things that do not make sense. Tony Stark started the plot by making a killer device named EDITH. Huh? I thought Stark stopped with these murder devices. And why give it to Peter? Why not someone he knew better, such as Pepper or Happy? And why the heck would he give them to Mysterio? I know you see him as a hero, Spider-Man, but you just met him! Not only do these not make sense, but they are incredibly frusturating.  Now, for why it is still good, despite its mistakes. The film is incredibly hilarious. Every single joke, especially when Nick Fury says “\*\*\*\*\*, please! You’ve been to space,” I laugh pretty hard. That is not even a funny line, but the way he says it always gets a laugh out of me. But at the same time, there is a lot of heart in the picture. I cry in happiness whenever the three have each other’s backs. The post credits scene is also when the greatest moment of the movie happens: Peter gets exposed because it leans into the third movie perfectly. But my favorite aspect of the plot is its romanticism. What I always say is if a movie has rushed romance, it has bad romance. Fortunately, I love how the film paces it well, does not cram it into one movie, and gives us the perspectives of both characters. **VERDICT: 14/20**\
**Visuals**
===========

# If there is one thing I love about the visuals of Jon Watts’ trilogy, it is that they keep improving. And proof of that is with Far From Home being an improvement over the original. It does not look great, but I must say that for some things, the MCU actually learned their lesson. Two things that they improved with is the color scheme and the shots. For the color palette, I think that the film’s use of aqua green is essential for representing mystery to the story. I know this because it is used with Mysterio, who we know little about for a long period of time into the movie. And the action scenes learned their lesson and actually have great shots. I think the best one is when Spider-Man punches Beck’s face and it turns into bricks. That is one of the best shots in any 2019 superhero movie. But improved does not always mean good. The film still has way too much CGI, especially for the elementals. I mean, did the crew ever hear of practical filmmaking? And at the same time, Peter’s “Night Monkey” suit looks very off-putting. It makes young Mr. Parker look like a bandit, not because of the color, but because of the texture of and gadgets attached to the suit. And he is anything but a bandit, come on! And finally we have the cinematography. It just looks like a bunch of weak shots outside of the action scenes that never look impressive. My favorite of said shots is when Peter and Beck shake hands. But otherwise, the visuals are fine, but I will praise them for being an improvement. **VERDICT: 11/20**

# **Characters**

# Look, not all of the characters are executed well. There are some I will get to that I personally think were done horribly dirty here. But for those that were executed well, they were executed with flying colors. Peter Parker is our main protagonist, once more. Peter still must keep his identity a secret from the public, so it creates some high stakes. He consistently helps others in need, so that creates a likable protagonist. He makes mistakes that keep him from perfection, so that creates a flawed protagonist. He has the makings of a fantastic protagonist, and I am so glad they actually did him right. MJ and Ned are his ride or dies. They are so enjoyable with their support with Pete, and have a very realistic banter with him. They have fun together, they get mad at each other, they consistently have each other’s backs. And I especially love how Peter is enthusiastic with the relationship, but MJ is skeptical. This grounds the film and reminds us we do not live on Disney Channel. Mysterio is the twist antagonist with zero foreshadowing, and why did they have to rip him off of Syndrome? This is an Incredibles ripoff because he got rejected by his idol and now hates superheroes, so he pretends to be one. Also, his defeat was just… did he live or die? Because he could still be out there. Aunt May and Happy Hogan are in this film. Did anyone remember that, because I sure did not. I felt like they were just there to be weird. One of them belongs in a different movie, and the other one is just there to annoy her nephew on accident. Lastly, Nick Fury and Maria Hill are there to ground Peter into reality, and even if Nick Fury seriously got done dirty after The Winter Soldier, he still can’t have a good movie every now and then, though that is rare. They both do their jobs well, and actually contribute to the story. They give Peter the motivation to trust Beck, and while it was done stupidly, better than them just not adding to the story. **VERDICT: 14/20**

# **Acting**

# You know, the acting is one of two categories I know honors the rest of the Infinity Saga. Tom Holland is Peter “Spider-Man” Parker. Holland does a fantastic job at still feeling like a kid, but at the same time, he has this Everyman Heroism factor in his body movement. Zendaya and Jacob Batalon are MJ Watson and Ned Leeds. The two are the definition of enjoyable and have excellent chemistry with Holland. At the same time, they have their falling outs, and it feels real. Jake Gyllenhaal is Mysterio. Gyllenhaal is an overrated actor in my opinion, but if he gives off a great portrayal, I am there for it. And this is one of those times. I do not like Quentin Beck as a character, but Gyllenhaal puts in a lot of mystery, yet fear factor into him. Samuel L. Jackson and Cobie Smulders are Nick Fury and Maria Hill. L. Jackson and Smulders are the most serious actors in this movie, and they do a fantastic job at it. I perfectly get their no-nonsense persona, yet they never overact. I honestly think that this is the weakest acting in the 4 Tom Holland Spider-Man movies, but it is still wonderful. **VERDICT: 20/20**

# **Music**

# Honestly, the music is absolute fire, and I feel like it never gets the praise it deserves. First off, the film sneaks in “Back In Black” by AC/DC, to honor Tony Stark. I appreciate how the film does not just let go of him, but at the same time, it fits the film’s creative side perfectly. As well, “I Will Always Love You” by the late Whitney Houston plays during the presentation of the deaths of famous deceased Avengers. I straight up cried during the beginning. But my favorite licensed song is the one that captures the feeling of the story the most, which is “Vacation” by The Go-Go's. Another reason is because Michael Giacchino brings back the heroic main Spider-Man theme, expands the Peter and MJ romance theme with “Bridge and Love's Burning"  and introduces a bold, futuristic electronic-and-brass theme for Mysterio. But I think that standout orchestral tracks include "Far From Home Suite Home" and various action cues designated to European locations like Venice, Prague, and London. And lastly, the action cues are still there and consistently work to let you know when the fighting scenes will happen. **VERDICT: 20/20**

# **To conclude**, Spider-Man 2: Far From Home is a good movie. It is not precisely as good as the first or its sequels, but it did stick the landing as an epilogue of Feige’s Marvel’s first ever completed saga. If you liked the first one, you should check this one out, and you might like it! **Final Score: 78%**
