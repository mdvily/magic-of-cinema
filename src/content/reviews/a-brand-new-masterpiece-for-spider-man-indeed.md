# **Spider-Man: Brand New Day (2026)**. May I just say that the MCU after Avengers: Endgame (2019) has been rough. Yes, not all of the movies were bad. I will even say that films such as Spider-Man: No Way Home (2021) and Thunderbolts\* and The Fantastic Four: First Steps (2025) were great. But the TV shows, other than Loki (2021-2023) were… mostly missed potentials. That, and the saga has not been building up to any new major threat, since phase 6 started. However, in terms of quality, the MCU is officially back from the dead. In my opinion, the last three MCU released movies were masterpieces. And Jon Watts is no longer directing the Tom Holland movies. That honor goes to Destin Daniel Cretton, who you may remember as the lead filmmaker from Shang-Chi and the Legend of the Ten Rings (2021). But as Tom Holland swings into a more serious role as Spider-Man, does he have what it takes to web itself to masterpiece status, as it did the $2 billion mark? Now, since this is a new movie, I will try to exclude spoilers.

# **Story**

# What the film does best is in storytelling. The film has this premise: after deleting his memory from existence, Peter Parker must fight a new telepathic enemy, before his mutated webs can turn him into something worse. That is a very mature synopsis, and it goes a long way into making you care about Peter Parker. As Roger Ebert said, “Movies are like a machine that generates empathy”. So if the film fails to take its time to let the emotional scenes hit harder, the machine malfunctions. This means that since the machine here works, there is a powerful sense of heart. For the scenes where Ned and MJ have Déjà vu of who their friendly neighborhood Spider-Man is, it hits hard, because the film builds up to it. This leads to a very powerful film that takes itself seriously. Also, it deals with themes such as depression and humanity. Yes, the depression aspect was already in Thunderbolts\* (2025). But the film breathes new life into it, by making Peter actually have to deal with his spider-sense going out of control as well. This helps make the film very unpredictable. But there is also a lot of comedy, mostly with The Punisher cursing like a sailor. It is very funny, and the dialogue goes a long way to make it feel real. **VERDICT: 20/20**

# **Visuals**

# Spider-Man: Homecoming (2017) and Far From Home (2019) have a very big problem. They look very artificial. Most notably, how the spider suit looks less like the team worked on the costume and more like they just used unnecessary CGI. Fortunately, No Way Home fixed that, and Brand New Day swung Spidey’s look into new heights. First off, the textures look exactly like how a New York City of the 2020’s would look. You have apartment buildings, police stations, vehicles like cars and vans, everything. This means that the setting is visually utilized very well, because the film takes full use into what New York City should be like. The cinematography, done by Brett Pawlak, is also spectacular. The film has mostly establishing shots for the action scenes and eye-level shots for conversations. The establishing scenes make you hooked into the scale of the action, and the eye-level shots make you feel like you are in these scenes. It makes you hooked on Peter’s journey. The colors are mostly grey. This helps the film look like it has a more gritty tone. Yes, it looks ugly, but that works, because it mirrors Peter’s life. The action as well choreographed, and every fight scene looks incredible. Peter’s battle with The Hulk, for example, has iconic scenery, such as The Hulk clapping Spider-Man back. But my favorite aspect is the CGI. Mostly, the villain’s effects on brainwashing. It looks amazing, because it looks original, and believable. **VERDICT: 20/20**

# **Characters**

# The characters of The MCU Spider-Man movies are consistently great, and that is the case here. Spider-Man/Peter Parker is our main protagonist, and feels more human than ever before. Him becoming an actual spider makes the drama more enticing, and his journey more human. But the greatest aspect is that he learns a new lesson across the movie: he does not need to fight his enemies and great dangers alone. Then you have is the villain. While saying who she is would be spoiler category, I can say, her wanting someone dear back makes things more powerful. She is very complex, and actually ends with a character arc of letting go. I am glad they refused to cram it into one movie, but this was a solid start. Also, she is a great mirror to Peter. They both lost someone they love, but Peter did not act on it. The villain did. Peter’s main ally is The Punisher/Frank Castle. Castle starts off as a cynical, foul-mouthed jerk who only cares about getting things done. But by the end, he learns to care about others, and even save Peter’s life. In fact, one thing I love about the characters is that struggles make them slowly develop through these movies. Spider-Man’s former best friends are Michelle “MJ” Jones-Watson and Ned Leeds. It is utterly heartbreaking for Peter to lose them, and the film knows it. That is why we spend more time with them finding out who Spider-Man really is. You then have The Hulk/Dr. Bruce Banner. Banner gives us one of the most intense fight scenes of the Marvel Cinematic Universe this decade, and him being turned into an antagonist is very cool, yet heartbreaking. MacDonald "Scorpion" Gargan is a secondary antagonist. He has such a threatening demeanor for one scene, but also has a sense of fear of something else. This upps the ante, and makes the fear factor of this villain with telekinesis feel real. Bill Metzger is the head of Damage Control, and a great example of an “actual villain”. Yes, it was the villain’s choice to act upon it, but who kidnapped someone she loved? He did. Finally, the film features Detective Jean DeWolff. DeWolff is Spider-Man’s most trusted ally in the police department, and has an actual character arc of seeing Spider-Man as not just an ally, but a friend. There is also another character in the movie, but saying who it is would spoil the movie. However, the character was very charming and actually had a great banter with Spidey. **VERDICT: 20/20**

# **Acting**

# Tom Holland is Spider-Man. Now, normally. Holland would put in a comedic portrayal. And while you will find some of that here, he mostly takes himself very seriously. We see him have straight faces, and say his lines like he cares about the situation. Sadie Sink is the villain. Sink feels sinister at the start, but she lets you see the sympathy in her character by the end. Jon Bernthal is Frank Castle. Bernthal makes his character cocky and arrogant at the start, but in the end, he gives him a sense of kindness and fragility. This makes Castle’s character arc more noticeable. Zendaya and Jacob Batalon are MJ and Ned Leeds. The two have excellent chemistry with both each other, and with Tom Holland. All while sounding and moving like actual early adults. Mark Ruffalo is Dr. Bruce Banner. My mother does not like Ruffalo in this role that much, let alone at all, but I do. I think he nails the intelligence and feeling like a teacher, and the terrifying nature of Hulk when he goes green. Michael Mando is a secondary antagonist of Scorpion, and he sounds threatening, yet fearing of something else. Doing that… is not cakewalk. Tramell Tillman is Bill Metzger, the director of Damage Control. You really get the feel like he is just trying to take care of his company, and put away criminals, thanks to Tillman’s performance. Lastly, Liza Colón-Zayas is Detective DeWolff. She sounds like an actual ally to Parker, and has a great maternal instinct to… yeah, no spoilers means no spoilers. **VERDICT: 20/20**

# **Music**

# Right off the bat, the music is composed by Michael Giacchino. I love a lot of his work, so he had big shoes to fill. And he was able to walk them to the end of the multiverse and back, because this is some of his best work in years. Now, first things first, the music supports the tone very well. The film is more down-tuned, which is a great mirror to the high-tuned of the Jon Watts trilogy. What I mean is that it sounds more serious, which complements the tone a very long way. Another thing I like about the music is that it cleverly adds in older themes to mirror Peter's lost history, while blending large orchestral swells with the action and montage scenes. I honestly could name how amazing the licensed songs are, but that gives it away. But what I can say is that they do an amazing job at demonstrating where Peter is in life, and what he feels. But my favorite track? Suite New Day. It sounds depressing, yet exciting. That is hard to do. **VERDICT: 20/20**

# **In conclusion**, Spider-Man: Brand New Day (2026) is an absolute triumph of a Spider-Man movie, and my new favorite. It has everything you would want from a movie about the webslinger in a red and black-webbed suit: comedy, action, a great villain, a likable Spidey, a cohesive story with humorous comedy, everything. If you like Spider-Man, this is the [#1](#1) movie you have to see, but even if you hate him, I would still recommend this feature. **Final Score: 100%**
