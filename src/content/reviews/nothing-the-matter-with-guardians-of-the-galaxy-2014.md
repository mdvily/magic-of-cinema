Greetings. I am setting out to review all of the MCU movies before Avengers: Doomsday (2026). For the list, I started out with Iron Man (2008). The next film on the docket is Guardians Of The Galaxy (2014). This film has always been one of the most acclaimed of the entire MCU. But does it deserve it? **SPOILERS!
Story**
=======

# When I see Guardians Of The Galaxy Volume 1, I see an underdog story. Yes, the film focuses more on action, but the film is about five characters despised by society, and so they prove to be more than what they are conceived to be by the end. Yes, this was done with Captain America: The First Avenger (2011), but it is different because in that movie, Steve Rogers proved to be worthy on a power scale. With the guardians, they succeed by improving morally. Therefore, the pacing would need to be spot-on. And sure enough, it absolutely is! Every scene moves decently quickly. While, yes, that sounds like a bad thing, but it works here. This is because it matches the nature of the guardians themselves. That, and it still feels appropriate. For instance, the core cast escapes the Kyln 15-17 minutes after the movie runtime reveals them as jailbirds there. This works because the prison is an important aspect of the story, but it is not the entire story. The pacing helps to make the character development feel earned, because of that. Another thing that I love about the story is how unpredictable it is. I mean, who knew that Peter Quill was a half-alien? Because yes, his father was entirely one, but we never knew that! In fact, the only information we knew about him was when Mrs. Quill tells her son, “You're so like your daddy. You even look like him. And he was an angel, composed outta pure light.” Another thing I never saw coming was that Ronan worked for Thanos this whole time. It works because it establishes him as an even bigger threat in the Marvel Cinematic Universe. My third praise is the message of overcoming your trauma and choosing your family. That is something the MCU has never done before. The films hard.y focus on family, and never think about forming one, not even with the avengers. So I found that unique. Speaking of which, the movie tends to shy away from the rest of the MCU. It was the first one to take place in outer space, the first one where the villain is no human or god, and the first one with no romantic kiss scene. But my favorite part is the tone. The heart is very strong, because the characters start to grow fond of each other, and use it to become better people. But the comedy? This is easily the funniest movie of the entire MCU. Now, first watching it for the Avengers: Doomsday marathon, I thought that it would be tonally inconsistent, but no. The drama is the plot, and the comedy is from the characters. The writers sometimes even use that comedy to add on to the story! For instance, Peter’s “Dance off, bro! Me and you.” joke is hilarious, makes sense for Quill to be saying it, because that is his character, and serves as a distraction to defeat Ronan. **VERDICT: 20/20**

# **Visuals**

# This movie is absolutely beautiful! I struggled to believe everything I saw in this movie alone, and that says something. First off, the color palette is vibrant from start to finish, and really complements the tone. The feature takes on colors from across and beyond the rainbow. For instances, the “Come And Get Your Love” segment is purple, Yondu’s face is blue, and the Kyln is orange and yellow. That is color grading at its finest, and even the villain colors are not a boring gray. Just shades of black and dark blue. That is how you know Kevin Fiege’s Marvel Universe had learned their lesson from: Thor 2: The Dark World! Then you have the textures. Every set feels lived in, with great detail, appropriate lighting. The lighting is amazing. Every scene is appropriately light or dark. The “Ain’t No Mountain High Enough” segment is bright to symbolize a happy ending, and Thanos’ space chair scene is dark to symbolize a threat coming among the universe in the future. The shots are incredible. We get to see so many marvelous shots, such as the guardians holding hands, or Peter holding Gamora. These have always been in my memory. The cinematography by Ben Davis is amazing. There are so many close-up shots that reflect to us what the characters are thinking. But my favorite aspect is easily the action. While the segments do not match up to the second and third, but they are incredible on their own. The escape from Morag sets up Peter’s flaw of cockiness, and sets up the story nicely. The Xandar brawl makes sense for the characters with unique choreography. The escape from the Kyln is humorous and has well-done cinematography. Knowhere is the most intense fight in the film with the best cinematography, and the battle for Xandar is the most intense and perfectly wraps up the character arcs. **VERDICT: 20/20**

# **Characters**

# Honestly, the characters are the only aspect of the movie I do not find perfect, but they are extremely close to it. If that does not say how good the movie is, what does? Peter “Star-Lord” Quill is the main protagonist/leader of the guardians. He has a very clear character arc of making peace with his mother’s death, which is very well paced. At the same time, his wacky antics are always a blast to watch. Gamora is next of the list, and she has an even better character arc of escaping Thanos’ clutches. She starts to realize that there is more family than just her adoptive father. Then we have Rocket. Rocket is the funniest of the bunch, especially with him thinking the “Raccoon” term is prejudice. He as well, has a strong and well paced character arc of realizing to use his intelligence for good. But he would need to do something awful for his arc to feel that awful and he does! Escaping 22 prisons, of course. Drax the destroyer has another arc of realizing that he does not need revenge. He goes from wanting to kill Ronan to realizing that all he needs is a new family after The Accuser killed his wife and daughter. Groot finally becomes the heart of the team. And a key example is when he sacrifices himself for Star-Lord, Gamora, Rocket and Drax. Rest in peace, Original Groot. I will talk more about Nebula and Yondu in the next movies, but they were great side villains here. Finally, there is one more character we need to talk about: Ronan The Accuser. Ronan is an adequate villain. He does not have much personality, but he does work as an underrated threat. I mean he did beat Drax with no effort. **VERDICT: 19/20**

# **Acting**

# Thank god the acting is not slept on, because it is absolutely amazing. Chris Pratt is Peter “Star-Lord” Quill. Pratt has this insane amount of charisma and enjoyability in his character, but you can also see trauma in his face. Zoe Saldana is Gamora. Saldana starts off with saying her lines coldly, but grows to have a more calming gesture, affecting the character growth. And at the same time, she has a surprisingly steady hand for holding the knife. Bradley Cooper is Rocket Raccoon. For Cooper’s performance, I want you to imagine Star-Lord’s portrayal by Pratt, and multiply it by two. And surprisingly, it works! It perfectly matches Rocket’s character and is just a mere joy to watch. Dave Bautista is Drax The Destroyer. Bautista for me perfectly comes between terrifying and humorous. Yes, his body movements scream “I want to kill someone”, but at the same time his voice is humorous. Vin Diesel is Groot. Now, I do not like Diesel as an actor that much, so I was pleasantly surprised to say that I actually enjoyed his performance. He really is expanding his roots without saying “Family”. Lee Pace is the villain Ronan. Now, while I am not the biggest fan of Ronan The Accuser as a villain, I must say Ronan’s anger is well executed with some serious personality. **VERDICT: 20/20**

# **Music**

# I swear, this movie has the best music in the entire Marvel Cinematic Universe. First off, I thought for some reason, it would be too loud, and in fact: some of the scenes are very quiet. Especially when Peter sees his mother’s final note. The silence perfectly shows us how emotional the scene is. Also, it affects the story very well. The loud tracks affect the action scenes and the silent tracks with the softer segments. The sound effects are also well done. Especially with the power stone effects. But my favorite aspect of the music is the licensed songs. "Come and Get Your Love" is a bop to listen to. The second it started playing, I could not get enough of the movie. “Fooled Around and Fell in Love" is the most romantic, and serves appropriate for Peter and Gamora’s relationship. "Hooked on a Feeling" gets me hooked on the feeling on what it is like to be in the Kyln. Lastly, "Ain't No Mountain High Enough" and "I Want You Back" are perfect conclusion songs. This is because they are highly optimistic, just like the conclusion. **VERDICT: 20/20**

# **In conclusion**, Guardians Of The Galaxy (2014) is an absolute masterpiece. Guardians is a film that consistently proves to be a better film than it has any right to be. So if you love superhero movies, this is a must-watch. **Final Score: 99%**
