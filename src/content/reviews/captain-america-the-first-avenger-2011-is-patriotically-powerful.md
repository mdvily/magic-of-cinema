# Greetings. I am setting out to review all of the MCU movies before Avengers: Doomsday (2026). For the list, I started out with Iron Man (2008). The next film on the docket is **Captain America: The First Avenger (2011)**. This movie is the MCU’s birthplace of who Steve Rogers is. For years, people have slept on the film, but in recent seasons, the feature got a re-evaluation. But did it deserve it? **SPOILERS!**

# **Story**

# The story does an amazing job at being well-written, and has one of the best of the Marvel Cinematic Universe’s early days. I think the greatest part of the story is the stakes. There are plenty of action segments, that, while forgettable, make you think Steve might actually die. I started believing it so many times, because it feels real. It takes place in the 1940’s, so the Nazis being the villains made too much sense, and that is a good thing. Speaking of which, the 1940s aesthetic makes a lot of sense, as this decade was the birthplace of Captain America in the Marvel comics timeline. Also, Johnston blends that with retro-future sci-fi very well. Lastly, the bittersweet ending of Steve losing Peggy for what we believe is forever at the time is incredibly sad. However, the film blends that well with subtle, humorous quips. My only real problem is the story structure. Once Steve Rogers turns into Captain America, the film goes from an underdog story to a war movie without enough transition. **VERDICT: 18/20**

# **Visuals**

# The visuals are good, but some of the worst of phase one. What I do like about them is that there is an absurdly great attention to detail. You can clearly see everything you need on the clothing the characters wear, the color of the sky, the colors in general, everything. You perfectly know what is going on with the visuals alone. Also, the “Skinny Steve” method has aged quite well and believably. He still looks skinny in the first act, even if the actor was not. Then, the atmosphere is 1940’s, which perfectly matches the look of the time period. Also, the character design of Red Skull’s design looks great, and very comic book-accurate. However, I have two big flaws. One: the action is very forgettable and poorly choreographed. Like, ask me any single action scene. I cannot. Also, the color palette is dull. All we mostly see is shades of grey, and it looks pretty awful. But get past that, and there is some goodness inside the visuals. **VERDICT: 14/20**

# **Characters**

Honestly, the characters could be better, but for what they are, they are great. Our main protagonist is Steve “Captain America” Rogers. Rogers starts off as the bullying victim to many, and a person who gets rejected from the military. But what I love about him is that he refuses to give up. Every fail that happens to him makes him work even harder. His integrity, obedience and leadership makes you want to root for him, but his weakness is his stubbornness, making him feel human. Peggy Carter is his main contact with the military, and his later love interest. What I love about her is how sweet the romance between her and Steve is. They both get looked down upon and both battle uphill battles. This makes their relationship feel real and earned. James Buchanan “Bucky” Barnes is Steve’s best friend, and I love his loyalty. And at the same time, he is a complete heavy hitter. Johann “Red Skull” Schmidt is the main antagonist, and he is fine. He does not completely suck, and could have been worse because he is an actual threat to Rogers. However, he could have been better because his motivation is just “Take over the world”. Colonel Chester Phillips is basically Steve’s mentor, and he starts off stubborn and stern. However, he starts to get an admiration for Rogers, as he sees that he has extreme potential. The side villain is Arnim Zola, and he is very underutilized. He is incredibly weak and contributes nothing to the story. Lastly, Dr. Abraham Erskine is the one who invented the super-serum, and him both turning Steve Rogers into Captain America and Johann Schmidt into Red Skull sprinkles a lot of interesting lore into the story. **VERDICT: 17/20
Acting**
========

# What is there to say about the acting that has not already been said? It screams flawless the moment you hear a single sentence. Chris Evans is Steve “Captain America” Rogers. Originally, Mr. Evans was skeptical, and said no because Evans had already played another prominent Marvel superhero, of Johnny “Human Torch” Storm. However, Joe Johnston, the director, saw a perfect balance of charisma, vulnerability, and strength in him. And his vision was correct. Evans does a perfect job at both feeling like a leader and like an actual person. Hayley Atwell is Peggy Carter. Atwell has a lot of strength in her body movements, especially as she pulls the pistol out at Steve. However, you can clearly see warmth and concern for him in her voice. Sebastian Stan is James Buchanan “Bucky” Barnes. Stan has an excellent chemistry with Rogers, and gives Bucky his own badbutt personality of kindness and bravery. Hugo Weaving is Johann “Red Skull” Schmidt. Weaving has a lot of sheer evilness in his voice, and his eyes say a lot about what he wants: the tesseract. I know this because he looks at it a lot. Tommy Lee Jones is Colonel Chester Phillips. He perfectly has that strong attitude at the start and gets a kinder demeanor at the end, when he respects Steve’s effort and determination. **VERDICT: 20/20**

# **Music**

# The music for this film is honestly… great. I think that it did a great job of explaining who this star-spangled man is. I have very little gripes, honestly. I guess the music can be too loud, and the bass aspect can sound dated, but everything else is amazing. First off, the music is uplifting, so it works narratively in the film’s favor. This is because the film is inspiring, and the music gets it perfectly. Second, the main theme is merely a rousing, brass-heavy, and patriotic main march, which perfectly captures who Rogers is as a character: patriotic, brave and noble. I always love it when music works in the writing’s favor, and this is a key example. Second, the film laid the groundwork for The Avengers, Avengers 3: Infinity War, and Avengers 4: Endgame, because Alan Silvestri would also compose them in time. This was his birthplace in the MCU. For instant, the same action cue here would be when Captain America pulls off an epic feat 81-78 years later in the timeline. That is attention to detail. Lastly, "Star Spangled Man" is catchy and great at naturally telling us who Captain Rogers is. But the reason that makes it this good is how it sounds like a tribute to America. It sounds so classical, exactly like 1812 Overture (1882). **VERDICT: 17/20**

# **In conclusion**, Captain America: The First Avenger is a pivotal movie, and one of the greatest of Phase One. With some problems, it is not a “Star-Spangled Masterpiece”, but it is a great and very enjoyable action movie with solid writing, visuals, acting, and music. So if you like Captain America, you should check this out. **Final Score: 86%**
