# Greetings. I am setting out to review all of the MCU movies before Avengers: Doomsday (2026). For the list, I started out with Iron Man (2008). The next film on the docket is **Captain America 3: Civil War (2016)**. This movie is considered to be one of the best Captain America movies, and of the MCU. It is also my second favorite Captain America movie of the MCU. But why?

# **Story**

# The story here is a more complex approach. After the good wins in Avengers 1, Captain America 2: The Winter Soldier and Avengers 2: Age Of Ultron, the film makes you see them as losses. Consequently, the Avengers are forced to sign the accords and be mandated by the government. Cap’s team decides not to sign, and Iron Man’s team decides to sign. It is very complex on which team is right and which is wrong. On one hand, if the Avengers are not controlled, they might be too powerful and accidentally destroy the world. But on the other, what if the government stops them from saving it? I do not even know which side I am on. But the other reason I love the story is heart and heartbreak. Every time Captain America demonstrates loyalty, I start to get locked in to the story. Even Iron Man stays loyal to Peter Parker, which means that whatever side is in the wrong, they still want to do good things. That, and the characters in the end split up. I more or less prefer the Avengers together, so seeing them fall apart is heartbreaking. My only real gripe is that I kind of knew this was going to happen. I mean, there was no way the Avengers would just apologize to each other and take the exact same side. **VERDICT: 18/20**

# **Visuals**

# Why do the visuals look so good? And why do people say otherwise? Because I think that the visuals get slept on and heavily criticized which is unfair. I think the visuals are amazing for four reasons. One: This iconic airport fight uses bright, clear daylight instead of dark, muddy shadows. This allowed me to see every piece of action, color, and superpower clearly without losing the characters in messy CGI. Two: the choreography. The practical stunts are insane and I love how the camera moves with said stunts without giving the “Shaky-Cam” feeling. Three: But while the CGI is at a minimum, there still IS CGI. Such as Iron Man’s armor and Black Panther’s suit. And the effects look so tangible I thought they were real! This is because they have an insane attention to detail. Four: Whenever they de-age Robert Downey Jr., it makes me feel like he actually is younger than he actually was. Good job, Marvel! **VERDICT: 20/20**

# **Characters**

# The characters here are amazing. Steve “Captain America” Rogers is our protagonist, and I feel like he has a good reason to not sign the Sokovia accords. He genuinely feels that the Avengers need to be there to save the day and protect innocent people. He still feels like a phenomenal leader, and the conflict he has with Tony Stark feels real. Speaking of which, Tony “Iron Man” Stark is the most complex character here. Deep down, he wants to protect the innocent, but he wants to change the Avengers because he finds it their fault that Ultron killed so many innocents. He genuinely wants to fix what he caused, but he does so the wrong way. Every single Avengers team member had a reason to sign or not to sign the accords. Wanda did not want to, because then they could weaponize her, Sam Wilson refused because so did Captain America, Ant-Man and Hawkeye joined Cap, because they were inspired by him, and The Winter Soldier was known to be innocent of Vienna’s bombing. Now, onto those who did sign. Natasha signed because she thought the Avengers needed accountability and to be kept in line, James “War Machine” Rhodes signed because he naturally believed in serving the military and Vision did not want the world to turn against them. Spider-Man and Black Panther were also welcome additions into the MCU, and have good reasons to be on team Iron Man: Spider-Man lost his parents, and sees Stark as a father figure, and Black Panther wants vengeance against Bucky because he thinks he murdered his father, King T’Chaka. Lastly, Zemo is arguably the easiest MCU villain to feel bad for. He wants to “fix” the Avengers because a member of them created Ultron, who orchestrated the death of his family. That is great writing because it makes you feel bad for him and let you know the avengers are not perfect. My only nitpick is that there are far too many characters, and this causes it to just feel like Avengers 2½. **VERDICT: 18/20**

# **Acting**

# Chris Evans is Steve “Captain America” Rogers. Evans does a perfect job at both feeling like a leader and like an actual person, even in the sequel. He nails the empathy of his character, and has excellent chemistry with others, especially Downey Jr. Robert Downey Jr. is Tony “Iron Man” Stark. RDJ perfectly shows and does not tell his frustration, but his tone is always humorous, yet heartbreaking to watch. Scarlett Johansson is Natasha “Black Widow” Romanoff. Johansson is so charming, yet says her lines with real sincerity. She knows who her character is, and the body movements are casual turned into action-packed. Just like her character. Sebastian Stan is James Buchanan “Bucky” Barnes/The Winter Soldier. Stan is the definition of a badass in this role. But behind that tough facade, there is an actual amount of emotion in his role. Especially him trying to forgive himself for his mistakes. Anthony Mackie is Sam “The Falcon” Wilson. Mackie perfectly sells two things. Those two thing are his chemistry with Evans, and his undeniable compassion. Elizabeth Olsen is Wanda “Scarlet Witch” Maximoff. Olsen’s role requires her to be angry, and she delivers that in spades, but also has a lot of compassion in her tone and gestures. Tom Holland is Peter “Spider-Man” Parker. Holland is the definition of hilarious in this role, and once he started referencing movies at the airport battle, I lost it. Chadwick Boseman is Prince T’Challa. Boseman has been widely recognized for this role, and I think he deserves it. Boseman brings so much gravitas to T’Challa, and makes him feel human with his accent and every single gesture. Daniel Brühl is Helmut Baron Zemo. Brühl perfectly gets between the edge of being insane and heartbroken, but he handles it so maturely. **VERDICT: 20/20**

# **Music**

# If there is a mixed bag many people would give Captain America: Civil War, it would have to be the music. And honestly, why? I thought the film’s soundtrack was great! What I dislike the most is that Captain America has no main theme, which he would need to stand out, when everyone else has one. Also, the music can be far too loud. For example, when Black widow tells Steve and Bucky to go, I did not hear the word, only the music. But I feel like it is highly unfair to call it just “functional”. One: The writing and the orchestra work together so well. Every scene is tense, and Henry Jackman’s score accompanies it. Almost each and every character has a main theme. Zemo, for instance has a swirling motif, which gets you straight into his trauma. Two: the music is more grand. This makes sense because there are more avengers so the stakes are higher. Three: "Siberian Overture" and "Clash" are the highlights of the film for me. I find them the most action packed and tense, and keep me at the edge of my seat. **VERDICT: 16/20**

# **In conclusion**, Captain America 3: Civil War is an amazing continuation of Steve Rogers’ journey. It is filled with heartbreak, great music, phenomenal acting and music and a very complex story. I would recommend this to any fan of Steve Rogers or the MCU, despite any shortcomings. **Final Score: 92%**
